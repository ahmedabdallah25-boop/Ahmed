## 1. What changed, and why

The previous version of this pack was a second-person essay: *"you"* sign the loan, *"you"* pay the
interest. It's a legitimate format and it's what the Shorts winners used. But it does not hold a
cold viewer for fifteen minutes, because an essay has no unresolved question in it — only
information, arriving in order.

The reference format solves that with **narrative**. Concretely:

> Two people, matched on every variable that could explain the outcome — same age, same income, same
> savings, same discipline. One difference: what they believe. Then follow them forward through
> escalating time checkpoints, stopping at each one to compare where they actually stand.

Why it retains: at every moment there is an open question — *who's winning?* — and it does not
resolve until the end. The viewer isn't consuming information; they're watching a race. The
checkpoints are the retention architecture, and they're load-bearing: each one both pays off the
previous open loop and opens a new one.

Three structural properties of the reference worth copying exactly:

1. **The wrong person is winning for most of the video.** The one whose choice *feels* right leads
   at the early checkpoints. That's what makes the reversal worth waiting for.
2. **Costs are revealed progressively, not stated up front.** The reference introduces a leader's
   cash flow as looking great — and flags that it only *looks* that way. The hidden costs then
   arrive one checkpoint at a time. Never dump the true cost early.
3. **There is a deliberate non-resolution at the midpoint.** Around the ten-year mark the reference
   explicitly says neither investor has clearly won. That is a retention device, not a hedge — it
   resets the open question at exactly the point where viewers leave.

And the ending refuses to declare a universal winner. It lands on *it depends, and here is what it
depends on*. For a finance channel this is worth more than a clean verdict: it's the thing that
makes the next video credible.

## 2. The Parallel Lives structure

Every episode. Timings are for a 15-minute runtime; scale proportionally.

| Act | Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Meet them** | 0:00–1:00 | Introduce two matched characters | Same age, same income, same amount of money, same discipline. State all four out loud — the matching *is* the hook, because it eliminates every excuse in advance. End on the promise: follow them N years, the outcome isn't what you'd expect. |
| **2. The fork** | 1:00–2:45 | Each makes their move | Concrete and specific: exact amounts, exact terms. Give each one a *belief*, not just an action — one wants something he can touch; the other wants something that doesn't need him. |
| **3. Day one** | 2:45–3:45 | Who looks like they're winning | Always the one who'll lose. Give the leader visible, social, emotional proof — the thing friends ask about. Give the other one a number in an app. End the act naming the asymmetry: one feels real, one feels invisible. |
| **4. Checkpoint: early** | 3:45–6:00 | **First scoreboard** | Year 3 or equivalent. The obvious winner is still ahead. Introduce **one** hidden cost here and let it sit. |
| **5. Checkpoint: middle** | 6:00–8:15 | **Second scoreboard** | The hidden costs compound and multiply. First real doubt. The leader's advantage narrows. |
| **6. Checkpoint: parity** | 8:15–10:15 | **Third scoreboard + deliberate non-resolution** | Near-dead-heat. Say out loud that neither has clearly won. This is the midpoint hold and it is mandatory. Mid-roll ad goes here. |
| **7. The hinge** | 10:15–11:15 | The thing neither of them saw | The structural reveal — the lend-vs-own question underneath the whole race. Not religion yet. Design. |
| **8. Checkpoint: final** | 11:15–12:30 | **Final scoreboard** | The payoff number. Full separation. |
| **9. The objection** | 12:30–13:30 | Steelman | Argue the losing side as strongly as it deserves and concede what's true. Non-negotiable. |
| **10. The third path** | 13:30–15:00 | **Bilal enters** | A third column appears on the scoreboard: the person who did it the interest-free way. Run his number against both. This is where the Islamic finance content lives — and because it arrives as a *third competitor in a race the viewer is already invested in*, it lands as a payoff instead of a lecture. |
| **11. Close** | 15:00–15:30 | Refuse the clean verdict | Name what it actually depends on. Tease the next race. Signature. |

**The rule that survives from the old format, unchanged:** the first ten minutes are universal money
mechanics. A viewer who has never heard the word *riba* must feel the whole race was run for them.
No Arabic term before Act 10. No `(X Explained)` in the first four words of a title.

**What the third path is for.** Not to declare Bilal the winner — sometimes he isn't, in pure dollars,
and the episodes say so. He exists to show that the trade-off the first two characters were arguing
about had a third option neither considered, with a different risk structure. Honesty here is the
whole value proposition of the channel.

## 4. The visual system

Five reusable devices. Once built, every episode is assembled from them — which is what makes a
weekly cadence survivable.

### DEVICE 1 — The Split Frame *(the signature)*

The frame divided by a thin vertical charcoal line. **Adam always left, Sami always right.** Never
swap sides — the audience learns the geography and can read a comparison in half a second. Both
halves always show the same moment in time.

> **SPLIT FRAME TEMPLATE:** Wide 16:9 composition on warm cream, divided into two equal halves by a
> single thin vertical charcoal line running floor to ceiling. Left half: [ADAM scene]. Right half:
> [SAMI scene]. Both halves lit identically, same scale, same eye level.

### DEVICE 2 — The Scoreboard Card

The checkpoint payoff. **Identical layout every single time** — that repetition is what lets a viewer
absorb a comparison in three seconds by episode two.

> **SCOREBOARD TEMPLATE:** Full-frame cream card with a thin charcoal border. A heading strip across
> the top. Below it, two equal columns divided by a thin vertical rule, each with a small circular
> character portrait at the top. Each column contains four horizontal line-item rows separated by
> hairlines, with a label on the left and a value box on the right. A heavy horizontal rule near the
> bottom, and beneath it one large TOTAL figure per column. The higher total's box is outlined in
> deep teal, the lower in brick red. No character in frame.

In Act 10 the same card gains a **third column** for Bilal — visibly narrower, sliding in from the
right, which makes his arrival a visual event.

### DEVICE 3 — The Timeline Ribbon

A persistent strip along the bottom 8% of frame: a horizontal line from YEAR 0 to the end year, with
tick marks and a filled marker showing where in the story you are. Present in **every** shot from
Act 2 onward.

Long-form's biggest silent killer is the viewer not knowing how much is left. The ribbon answers it
continuously and costs nothing.

> **RIBBON TEMPLATE:** A slim horizontal strip across the bottom edge of frame on cream, containing a
> thin charcoal baseline with evenly spaced small tick marks, a filled deep-teal segment running from
> the left end to the current position, and a small solid circular marker at that position.

### DEVICE 4 — The Hidden Cost Card

Whenever a cost is revealed, a small rust-coloured card slides in over the leader's half of the split
frame and a figure is struck through and rewritten lower. Always the same animation. By the third one
the audience feels dread when they see the card shape appear — which is exactly right.

> **HIDDEN COST TEMPLATE:** A small rounded rust-terracotta card with a thin darker border overlapping
> the lower-left of frame, containing one label line and one figure, with a brick-red strike-through
> line across a larger figure above it and a smaller figure written beneath.

### DEVICE 5 — The Ledger Wall *(retained)*

Kareem's presenting space, used between checkpoints for the arithmetic.

> **SET BLOCK — LEDGER WALL:** A large flat cream wall covered edge to edge with a faint hand-drawn
> grid of thin charcoal lines, a few chalk-style numbers and bar shapes sketched on it, a plain
> narrow shelf running along the bottom.

**Running motif:** the wall accumulates across the season. Episode 2 opens with episode 1's final
number still faintly sketched in the corner. By the finale it carries all five.

### Supporting sets

**THE TWO HOUSES** — two identical simple homes side by side, one on each half of the split frame,
where Adam and Sami live their parallel lives.
**THE COUNTER** — a plain institutional counter with a grille and one featureless grey silhouette
behind it. Grey silhouettes are always institutions; only the four named characters have faces.

## 5. Pacing and retention

- **~60–70 distinct shots per episode.** Average 13–15 seconds. Faster than the previous format
  because the split frame lets you change one half while holding the other.
- **Scoreboard cards hold longer** — 6–8 seconds. They're the payoff; let them land.
- **The emotional beats are not filler.** The reference spends real time on how each path *feels*:
  driving past the property and thinking *I own that*; friends asking about one investment and never
  the other. Copy this. It is what makes the arithmetic land, and it is the single biggest difference
  between a video that retains and a spreadsheet read aloud.
- **Every spoken figure appears on screen within 0.3s.** Large, upper third.
- **Mid-roll at the Act 6 non-resolution**, immediately after "neither of them has clearly won."
- **Captions mandatory.** Upload an SRT; `hasCaption: false` was a documented failure on the sister
  channel's long-form.

## 6. Packaging

**Title formula for this format:** the matched-pair setup, or the gap.
> `Two Men Bought the Same Car. One Paid $8,000 More.`
> `Same Debt, Same Card, 16 Years Apart`
> `One Bought, One Rented, One Partnered`

Numbers beat adjectives. No Arabic term in the first four words.

**Thumbnail system** — one template, so the channel is recognisable in a sidebar:

- **Split down the middle**, matching the video's own signature device.
- **Adam left, Sami right**, both from mid-thigh up, facing each other slightly.
- The contrast object beside each — one solid, one ghost outline; a tall stack and a short one.
- **Three words maximum**, heavy sans-serif charcoal, top centre spanning the divide.
- One brick-red element only. Must read at 168px.

**Description template**

```
[One sentence: the matched setup and the gap.]
[Two sentences: what the video follows and where it lands.]

⏱ CHAPTERS
0:00 Meet [Adam] and [Sami]
...

▶ Watch next: [link]     🔗 Series playlist: [link]

Financial education, not financial advice. All figures illustrative and rounded —
assumptions listed above. Run your own numbers.
```

**Pinned comment, every episode:** *"Which one were you — Adam or Sami? And be honest."* Identity
questions outperform advice questions by a wide margin, and this one costs nothing.

## 7. The funnel — still the thing that decides whether any of this is seen

`longform-fix.md` records a 5-minute video published into a 191-sub Shorts audience sitting at **2
views**. @DeenAndMoney starts from **0 subscribers**. Long-form gets no Shorts-feed distribution and
Browse impressions for a new channel are measured in dozens.

So: **every episode ships with three Shorts cut from its own footage**, specified at the end of each
episode file. This format harvests better than the essay format did — a checkpoint scoreboard is
already a self-contained 50-second payload with a built-in hook.

Publish long-form first, then one Short per day for three days. **Do not publish episode 2 until
episode 1's three Shorts are live.**

## 8. Production pipeline

1. **Lock the arithmetic in a spreadsheet before writing a word.** Every episode file opens with the
   locked table. The script is written around the numbers, never the reverse.
2. **Generate the four-character reference sheet**, then pass it as an image reference on every shot
   alongside the written CHARACTER BLOCK. Text-only prompts drift within about six frames — with four
   characters this is now the single biggest consistency risk in the pipeline.
3. **Build the five reusable devices once**, as templates. Then each episode is populated, not
   designed from scratch.
4. **Generate in set batches** — all split-frame shots together, all scoreboards together.
5. **Record VO to the locked script, then cut images to VO.** Never the reverse.
6. **Harvest the three Shorts before publishing.**

**Per-episode QC**

- [ ] Both characters matched on every variable, stated aloud before 1:00
- [ ] The wrong one is winning at checkpoints 1 and 2
- [ ] Exactly one hidden cost introduced per checkpoint, never dumped early
- [ ] Explicit "neither has clearly won" at the midpoint
- [ ] Bilal does not appear before Act 10
- [ ] No Arabic term before 13:30
- [ ] Objection act present, with a real concession
- [ ] Ending refuses a universal verdict
- [ ] Runtime over 8:00 for mid-roll · chapters set · SRT uploaded
- [ ] Adam always left, Sami always right, in every frame and the thumbnail
- [ ] Timeline ribbon present from Act 2 onward
- [ ] Three Shorts cut and queued

## 9. Cadence and decision rules

**Weekly long-form + three Shorts on the following days.** The About page promises weekly; consistency
is the only Browse signal a new channel can manufacture.

**Slot:** treat the first four uploads as a test — two at 3:00 AM PT (the sister channel's proven
window) and two at 4:00 PM PT, then compare first-48-hour impressions.

**After the first three episodes have been live 14 days each:**

- **≥ 500 views/ep** → format works. Hold it, push runtime toward 16 minutes.
- **150–500** → format works, packaging doesn't. Retitle and re-thumbnail the weakest two.
- **< 150 with all Shorts live** → shift the ratio to five Shorts per long-form and pick the next
  three topics from whichever Shorts outperformed.
- **A Short beats its parent by 20×** → that Short's hook is the real title. Re-cut the cold open.

**Diagnostic order if two consecutive episodes underperform:** first four words of the title →
thumbnail legibility at 168px → the first 30 seconds. Topic is the last thing to change, not the
first.
