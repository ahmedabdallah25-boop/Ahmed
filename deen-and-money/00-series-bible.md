## 1. The long-form decision — and the one thing that will kill it

Long-form is the right call for this niche: the topics are arithmetic-heavy, the RPM in personal
finance is among the highest on YouTube, and a 12-minute video with a mid-roll earns multiples of
what a Short earns per viewer. Nothing below argues against it.

But `longform-fix.md` records exactly how this fails. The sister channel published one 5-minute
video into a 191-sub Shorts audience and it sat at **2 views**, because long-form gets zero Shorts-feed
distribution and Browse impressions for a small channel are measured in dozens. @DeenAndMoney starts
from **0 subscribers** — an even colder start.

So the funnel is not a follow-up task. It is part of the production of every episode:

**Every long-form episode ships with three Shorts cut from its own footage.** Each Short is a
self-contained 45–60s payload (they are specified at the end of each episode file), each ends on the
episode's open question, and each description leads with the full-episode link. The Shorts do the
job the algorithm will not do for you: they buy the impressions that create the subscribers that
create the Browse signal that finally lets long-form breathe.

Publish order per episode: **long-form first**, then one Short per day for the next three days. The
long-form accumulates watch time while the Shorts feed it.

**Do not publish episode 2 until episode 1 has its three Shorts live.** A back catalogue of
long-form with no clip layer is the 2-view outcome, repeated.

## 2. Positioning — why this channel is not the other channel

Two channels in one niche cannibalise each other unless they own different jobs.

| | Finance % Decoded | **Deen & Money** |
|---|---|---|
| Format | Shorts, 1.5–3 min | **Long-form, 11–13 min** |
| Job | *How the machine works* | *What it costs you, to the dollar* |
| Method | Mechanism diagrams | **Live arithmetic — one number, built on screen, step by step** |
| Proof standard | Explanation | A calculation the viewer can reproduce themselves |
| Viewer leaves with | Understanding | **A number about their own life, and a decision** |
| Signature | "No jargon, just mechanisms." | **"Run the numbers. Then run them halal."** |

**The channel's one visual signature:** every episode builds a single number on screen and returns to
it. It appears in the cold open, gets constructed piece by piece through the middle, gets challenged in
the objection act, and gets re-run under the Islamic structure at the end. The viewer should be able
to name that number after watching — that is the retention hook and the reason to subscribe.

**Audience:** anyone with a paycheck. Muslim or not. The Islamic finance content is the *answer*
this channel gives, never the topic it advertises. A viewer who has never heard the word *riba*
must feel the first eight minutes were made specifically for them.

## 3. The Long-Form Spine

Reverse-engineered from the sister channel's five 900+ view winners (Parts 8, 3, 9, 10, 4), and from
why Parts 11–12 stalled, then rebuilt for a 12-minute runtime. **Every episode obeys this.**

| Act | Time | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Cold open** | 0:00–0:35 | Stop the scroll | Second person, an impossible-sounding claim about the viewer's own money, and the core number spoken out loud. No logo, no "hey guys", no channel intro. |
| **1. The promise** | 0:35–1:20 | Buy 12 minutes | Say precisely what they'll be able to do at the end, and plant two open loops you'll close later ("there's a specific month where this flips — I'll show you which one"). |
| **2. The normal case** | 1:20–3:30 | Establish the world | The ordinary transaction, in plain terms. The viewer should recognise their own life. Introduce the number as a *question*, not an answer. |
| **3. The build** | 3:30–6:30 | Retention engine | Construct the number on screen, one component at a time. Each step lands a small surprise. This is the longest act and it must never plateau. |
| **4. The re-hook** | 6:30–7:00 | Survive the midpoint | Restate the stake in a new way at the ~50% mark, where the biggest drop-off sits. Close one open loop here. |
| **5. The objection** | 7:00–8:30 | Earn trust | Steelman the strongest counterargument in the comments before anyone types it — and answer it honestly, conceding what's true. This act is what makes a finance channel credible. |
| **6. The turn** | 8:30–9:15 | Pivot without preaching | "None of this is a law of nature. It's a contract design. Change the design and the arithmetic inverts." Announce *structure*, never religion. |
| **7. The dive** | 9:15–12:00 | Deliver the reason to subscribe | Islamic finance, in full: the underlying principle → the named contract → how it actually works → **the same number re-run** → how to do it this month → the honest downsides. |
| **8. The close** | 12:00–12:40 | Convert | Recap the number in one sentence, tease the next episode's number, signature line, one clear CTA. |

**Non-negotiables**

- The Islamic finance content starts at **Act 7 and not before.** Acts 0–6 are universal money
  mechanics. This is the exact shape of the breakthrough script, stretched.
- **Arabic terms:** introduced once, after the 9-minute mark, immediately followed by a plain-English
  definition. Never in the cold open. Never in the first line of a title. `(X Explained)` suffix only.
- **No series bookkeeping in the opening.** No "in the last episode…". That beat is what halved the
  sister channel's velocity on Parts 11–12, and at 0 subscribers ~100% of impressions are cold.
- **Act 5 is mandatory.** Skipping the objection is what makes explainer channels feel like sales
  pitches. Concede real ground: halal products often *do* cost more, have fewer providers, and
  sometimes underperform. Say so, then say why the trade is still worth it.
- **Never say "guaranteed", "risk-free", or "you should".** The channel teaches mechanisms and
  arithmetic. Every description carries: *financial education, not financial advice*.

## 4. Retention architecture

A 12-minute video lives or dies on the first 45 seconds and the 50% mark. Build these in:

- **Pattern interrupt every 15–20 seconds.** New shot, new framing, or a visual state-change. The
  storyboards below are built to this cadence — roughly 40 shots per episode.
- **Open loops.** Plant two in Act 1, close one in Act 4 and one in Act 7. Loops are the only thing
  that reliably carries a viewer across the midpoint.
- **The number is always visible.** A persistent corner counter showing the running total, updating
  as the build proceeds. Viewers stay to watch a number finish moving.
- **Chapter titles are hooks, not labels.** "Where the money actually goes", not "Part 2".
- **No recap of what you just said.** Long-form editors pad; padding is measurable in the retention
  graph. If a sentence doesn't add a number, an image, or a turn, cut it.
- **Mid-roll ad placement:** at the Act 4 re-hook (~6:45), immediately after a cliffhanger sentence.
  Runtime must exceed 8:00 for mid-rolls — every episode here does.

## 6. Recurring sets

A long-form series needs a *world*, not disconnected frames. Five sets, reused across all episodes so
the channel becomes visually recognisable. Paste the relevant SET BLOCK into any prompt using it.

**SET A — The Ledger Wall** *(the channel's signature space)*
> A large flat cream wall covered edge to edge with a faint hand-drawn grid of thin charcoal lines,
> a few simple chalk-style numbers and bar shapes sketched on it, a plain narrow shelf running along
> the bottom.

**SET B — The Desk**
> A plain wooden desk seen in three-quarter view, a simple lamp, a small stack of papers, a mug, a
> closed laptop, nothing else. Cream wall behind, no window.

**SET C — The Counter**
> A plain institutional counter with a simple grille or glass divider, a small speaking gap, one
> featureless grey silhouette figure standing behind it. Cream wall, no signage, no branding.

**SET D — The Long Road**
> A single straight road receding to a flat horizon under an empty cream sky, thin dashed centre
> line, occasional simple milestone posts at the roadside, no vegetation, no vehicles.

**SET E — The Kitchen Table**
> A plain round kitchen table with two simple chairs, a bowl and a folded newspaper on it, cream wall
> behind, warm domestic feel with no clutter.

**Running motif — the Ledger Wall.** Every episode opens and closes on SET A. In the cold open the
wall is blank except for one number. By the close, the wall carries the finished calculation. Across
the season, the wall accumulates: episode 2's cold open shows episode 1's number still faintly
sketched in the corner. It costs nothing and it makes five videos read as one series.

## 7. Titles, thumbnails, packaging

**Title formula.** `[Your thing] [does the harm]` or `How to [benefit] Without [pain]`. Second person
in the first four words. Numbers beat adjectives. Arabic term only in a `(X Explained)` suffix.

Test every title against the five winners' shape:
> "How to Buy a House Without Interest" · "How Banks Quietly Trap Your Savings" ·
> "Why Everything Costs More" · "How to Earn Passive Income Without Interest"

**Thumbnail system** — one consistent template so the channel is recognisable in a sidebar:

- Kareem on the **right third**, one clear expression from the library, cut off at mid-thigh.
- **Left two-thirds:** the episode's core visual contrast — two objects, one solid and one dashed
  ghost outline (two cars, two houses, two piles).
- **Three words maximum**, heavy sans-serif, charcoal on cream, occupying the top-left.
- One brick-red element only, placed on the thing you want the eye to hit first.
- Must read at 168px. If you can't identify the contrast at that size, it fails.

Each episode file carries its own thumbnail prompt.

**Description template**

```
[One-sentence hook restating the number.]

[Two sentences: what the video shows and the mechanism it lands on.]

⏱ CHAPTERS
0:00 [hook chapter title]
...

▶ Watch next: [next episode link]
🔗 Full series playlist: [link]

Sources and calculators used: [links]

This is financial education, not financial advice. All figures are illustrative
and rounded; run your own numbers before making any decision.
```

**Pinned comment:** always a question that invites the viewer's own number.
*"Run yours: what's the total you'd pay on your own [loan/mortgage/balance]? Drop the two numbers and
I'll do the arithmetic in the next video."* This is the single cheapest engagement lever available.

**Tags:** 8–12 per video, in this order — the universal money term first (`mortgage`, `car loan`,
`inflation`), the calculation term second (`amortization`, `compound interest`), then the Islamic
terms (`halal mortgage`, `murabaha`, `islamic finance`). Cold-audience terms lead.

## 8. Production pipeline

1. **Lock the number first.** Before writing a word, build the calculation in a spreadsheet. The
   script is written around the arithmetic, never the reverse.
2. **Generate Kareem's reference sheet** (prompt below), then pass it as an image reference on
   *every* shot alongside the written CHARACTER BLOCK. Text-only prompts drift on the beard and the
   henley collar within about six frames — this is the single most common consistency failure.
3. **Generate in set batches.** All SET A frames in one session, all SET B frames in the next.
   Switching sets mid-batch increases drift.
4. **Record VO to the locked script**, then cut images to the VO. Never the reverse — image-led
   editing is how 12-minute videos become 16-minute videos.
5. **On-screen text within 0.3s of the spoken number.** Every figure spoken must appear. Large, top
   third, high contrast — a large share of the audience watches muted.
6. **Captions are mandatory.** `hasCaption: false` was a documented failure on the sister channel's
   long-form. Upload an SRT; do not rely on auto-captions.
7. **Harvest the three Shorts** before publishing the long-form, so they're queued and ready.

**Reference sheet prompt**

> Character reference sheet on a warm cream background, three full-body views side by side labelled
> FRONT VIEW, 3/4 VIEW, SIDE VIEW, identical character in all three, neutral standing pose, arms
> relaxed at sides, soft elliptical ground shadow. [CHARACTER BLOCK] [STYLE BLOCK]

**Per-episode QC checklist**

- [ ] Core number stated aloud before 0:35
- [ ] No Arabic term before 9:00
- [ ] Act 5 objection present and honestly conceded
- [ ] Runtime over 8:00 for mid-roll eligibility
- [ ] Every spoken figure appears as on-screen text
- [ ] Chapters set, first one at 0:00
- [ ] SRT uploaded
- [ ] Thumbnail legible at 168px
- [ ] Three Shorts cut and queued
- [ ] Pinned comment ready
- [ ] Next-episode tease matches what you're actually making next

## 9. Publishing and decision rules

**Cadence:** one long-form per week, plus its three Shorts on the following three days. The About
page already promises weekly — keep that promise; consistency is the only Browse signal a new
channel can manufacture.

**Time slot:** the sister channel's audience is proven active at 3:00–4:30 AM PT. Long-form behaves
differently from Shorts, so treat the first four uploads as a test — publish two at 3:00 AM PT and
two at 4:00 PM PT and compare first-48-hour impressions before locking a slot.

**Decision rule — after the first 3 episodes have been live 14 days each:**

- **≥ 500 views/episode** → the funnel works. Hold the format, increase runtime toward 15 minutes.
- **150–500** → the funnel works, the packaging doesn't. Retitle and re-thumbnail the weakest two
  before making episode 6.
- **< 150 with all three Shorts live** → the topics are right but discovery isn't landing. Shift the
  ratio: five Shorts per long-form, and pick the next three topics purely from which Shorts
  outperformed.
- **A Short beats its parent long-form by more than 20×** → that Short's hook is the real title.
  Re-cut the long-form's cold open to match it.

**Diagnostic rule:** if two consecutive episodes underperform, the problem is almost never the topic.
Check in this order — the first four words of the title, the thumbnail's legibility at 168px, then
the first 15 seconds of the script. Topic is the last thing to change, not the first.
