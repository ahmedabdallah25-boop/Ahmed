WHAT THIS FILE IS

Two complete, separate ways to shoot this Short. Pick one — do not mix them,
the idioms don't cut together.

  TRACK A — VIDEO.  11 generated clips. Highest production value, highest cost,
                    most drift risk. Use while the Seedance trial is live.
  TRACK B — STILLS. 11 generated images, moved in the edit. A quarter of the
                    generations, no temporal artefacts, and the frames are
                    reusable as thumbnails and as b-roll on later episodes.

The image prompts are NOT the video prompts with the motion deleted. They are
written as single decisive frames — tighter, more still-life, lit harder, with
real lens language — because a still has to survive being looked at for four
seconds without moving. Composition differs too: stills are framed with more
negative space, since the edit push will crop into them.

The timings below are the real cut. They come from the rendered composition
(video/src/klarna/beats.ts), so they already match the VO in media/klarna-vo.mp3.

================================  TRACK B  =====================================
                        STILLS — 11 IMAGES + EDIT MOVES
================================================================================

SETTINGS: 9:16 · 1080x1920 (or 2048x3640 and downscale — you want the headroom
for the push) · one image per scene.

WHY THESE READ DIFFERENTLY. A still gets stared at. So these prompts trade the
video track's staged action for things a frozen frame can carry: raking light,
real surface texture, a decisive angle, and a single unambiguous subject. They
are framed 10–15% wider than the video prompts, because every one gets a slow
push in the edit and the push crops in.

CONSISTENCY: generate B03 first for the shopkeeper and B11 for AMIR, approve
both, lock the seed, and use them as image references everywhere the same figure
appears. With only 11 images you can afford to regenerate until each is right —
that is the main advantage of this track.

HOW TO MOVE THEM. Each still gets the move listed under it. Hold each for the
scene's full length from the map above. The channel's Remotion pipeline already
does this — drop the files in video/public/broll/ and drive them the way
src/inflation/ drives its stills. Push percentages are total travel across the
scene, eased out, never linear.
