### ACT 1 — THE MAGIC TRICK — 0:25–1:20

**VO:**
Walk into a bank and borrow twenty thousand dollars. Here's what most people think happens: the bank takes twenty thousand from someone's savings… and hands it to you. [pause] That is not what happens. [curious] Nobody's account goes down. The banker types two-zero-zero-zero-zero into your account — and *new money* appears. Money that did not exist one second earlier.

Don't take my word for it. In 2014, the Bank of England — the world's oldest central bank — published a paper admitting it in plain English: when a bank lends, it *creates* the deposit. [pause] Around ninety-seven percent of all the money in the economy was made exactly this way. Not minted. [whispers] Typed.

### ACT 2 — THE CATCH — 1:20–2:20

**VO:**
Now here's the part they didn't put in the paper's title. [pause] The bank created your twenty thousand… but you owe back twenty-*three*. [pause] So ask the obvious question: [curious] where do the extra three thousand come from? The bank never created them. Nobody did.

The only place that money can exist… is inside *someone else's* loan. [pause] Which means, system-wide, the debt is always bigger than the money. Always. It's musical chairs — every player scrambling for dollars that don't all exist — and the game only continues if new borrowers keep joining. [sighs] More loans. More debt. Every single year. Not because we're greedy. Because the machine *stalls* if we stop.

### ACT 3 — THE BILL — 2:20–3:20

**VO:**
So who pays for all this new money? [pause] You do. Just never as a bill with your name on it.

Every fresh dollar typed into existence quietly shrinks the ones already in your pocket. That's the inflation you feel at the grocery store — the tax you never voted for. And notice *where* new money lands first: banks, funds, asset owners. They buy before prices rise. Your salary shows up last… [sarcastic] after everything already costs more.

And when the machine finally choked in 2008? It didn't apologize. It sent the bill — seven hundred billion dollars — to the public that never got a vote. [pause] [whispers] You weren't bailed out. You were billed.

### ACT 4 — THE 1,400-YEAR-OLD RULESET — 3:20–4:25

**VO:**
Now — what if I told you there's a ruleset, fourteen hundred years old, engineered to make this exact trick impossible? [pause] Three rules.

Rule one: money cannot rent money. Charging for the *passage of time* — riba — is banned. No interest, no debt spiral by design. [pause] Rule two: every deal must tie to something *real* — a house, a car, a business. Money stays a measuring stick. It never becomes the merchandise. [pause] Rule three: risk is *shared*. The financier profits only when you do — and eats losses with you. No more heads-they-win, tails-you-pay.

[excited] That's not nostalgia. That's engineering. A machine that physically cannot print claims on your future — because every unit of value has to be anchored to something that actually exists.
