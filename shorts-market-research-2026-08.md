# Faceless Shorts market study — August 2026

Research pass run 2026-08-16. Sources: Nexlev channel/outlier database (30 channels pulled
through batch metrics, ~9,400 faceless Shorts channels filtered), vidIQ outlier search,
YouTube public channel listings, and transcripts of top-performing videos.

Scope filter applied as asked: **faceless only, monetized only.**

---

## 1. What I checked and what came back

I pulled three cohorts of 10 channels each through Nexlev's batch metrics, chosen from
different corners of the faceless Shorts market:

| Cohort | What it is | Monetized | Median views/video |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — viral curiosity/science | tiny channels with monster outlier videos | 7/10 | 654K |
| B — high-volume proven | 200–3,000 uploads, established | 9/10 | 1.43M |
| C — money/business | wealth, investing, business explainers | 7/10 | 1.16M |

**24 of 30 channels were monetized.** Faceless is not a monetization obstacle — that
question is settled and not worth spending more time on.

### The RPM data is thin — read it as directional only

Only **4 of 30 channels returned an RPM value**. Everything else came back null. Those four:

| Channel | Category | RPM |
|---|---|---|
| Thinknomy® | business / economics | **$5.39** |
| Heartsnreels | movies / commentary | $1.96 |
| Spider Zelok | music / entertainment | $1.93 |
| Wealth Trion | business / documentary | $1.77 |

I can't honestly claim "business RPM is 2× entertainment" off this — the two business
channels straddle the two entertainment ones. What the data *does* support: the single
highest RPM in the sample was an economics explainer, and no entertainment channel
cleared $2. Treat business/finance as *probably* the better-paying half, not proven.

## 2. The finding that actually matters

The interesting number isn't RPM. It's **views per video produced** — how many uploads it
takes to buy a hit. On that measure one channel dominates everything else I pulled:

### Under Six Minutes Studio (`@udsmv`) — 6.79K subs, 20 videos

| Video | Views |
|---|---|
| How Underwater Welding Works | **20M** |
| Survivorship Bias | **9.8M** |
| Soviet invincible nailing technique explained | 4.1M |
| The Evolution of Welding | 3.2M |
| Why Horseshoe Nails Are Shaped Like Chisels | 1.7M |
| Why Engineers Chose Rivets Over Welding in WW2 Aircraft | 1.5M |
| Why German Engineers Removed Sirens From Their Aircraft | 1.4M |

**7 of 20 videos over 1M. 35% hit rate. ~2.2M average views per video. 6,790 subscribers.**
Monetized. Faceless. Original 3D Blender animation — nothing borrowed.

Nothing else came close on efficiency. For contrast, Mind Fragments (10.2K subs) has
29 videos and 4 over 1M — a 13% hit rate — and its 10M-view winner turned out to be
repurposed found footage whose entire transcript is *"Aquí hay oxígeno. Wow."* Two words
of Spanish. That's not a format you can build on.

### The trap I'd steer you away from

**Infinite Wealth Lab** (164K subs) is the loudest performer in the money space — top
Short at **75M views**, and a wall of 5M–45M videos. I pulled the transcript of its
9.2M-view *"This Is How RICH PEOPLE Borrow MONEY 💼"*:

> "I'd like to open a line of credit… There's a $95 annual fee plus a credit limit of
> $10,000." / "I think I'm going to need a bit more than that." / "How much were we
> thinking?" / "50 million." / "You have $43,000 in your bank account."

That's a scene from *WeCrashed* — the Adam Neumann WeWork drama. The channel is
re-uploading TV clips with caps-lock titles. It's monetized today, but it's built on
someone else's footage and one policy sweep from zero. Its titles are worth stealing.
Its content model is not.

## 3. The niche I'd pick: **Money Mechanics**

Counterintuitive explainers about the hidden machinery of money — the Under Six Minutes
*format* pointed at the Infinite Wealth Lab *topic*.

Why this specific intersection:

- **The format has the best proven hit rate** in the whole dataset (35%), and it's fully
  original — no rights exposure, no reused-content risk.
- **The topic sits in the higher-RPM half** of the market, and it's the one category where
  the top RPM reading in my sample landed ($5.39).
- **It's faceless by construction.** Diagrams and animation *are* the medium, not a
  workaround for not showing a face.
- **The generic version is saturated slop, which is the opportunity.** vidIQ's outlier
  search for "money psychology / how rich people think" returned almost entirely
  1K–16K-sub channels posting 6-second "7 Money Habits" listicles at 0.2–2% engagement.
  Meanwhile the *specific mechanism* videos clear millions: "Why Rich People Never Wear
  Designer Logos" pulled 508K on a 6.6K-sub channel; "Why Rich People Hide Their Wealth
  in Denmark 🇩🇰" pulled 138K on a **393-sub** channel. Specificity is the whole moat.

Read against `channel-reset.md`: Finance % Decoded's diagnosed problem is topic
exhaustion, not packaging. This is a topic engine, which is the thing that was missing —
every "why does [specific actor] do [specific weird thing]" is a fresh video, and the
supply is effectively unlimited.

## 4. The title formula, taken off the winners

Every high performer in the sample is the same shape:

> **[Why / How] + [specific named actor] + [concrete counterintuitive behaviour]**
> — and the payoff is *withheld*.

- Why Carpenters **Blunt** Nail Tips (459K)
- Why Engineers Chose **Rivets Over Welding** in WW2 Aircraft (1.5M)
- Why Horseshoe Nails Are Shaped **Like Chisels** (1.7M)
- Why German Engineers **Removed** Sirens From Their Aircraft (1.4M)
- Why Rich People **Never Wear Designer Logos** (508K on 6.6K subs)

Rules that fall out of it:

1. **Never answer the question in the title.** The title is the open loop.
2. **Name a specific actor** — carpenters, German engineers, Delta, your bank. Not
   "people," not "you."
3. **The behaviour must sound wrong.** Blunting a nail sounds like a mistake. That
   friction is the click.
4. **One emoji, at the end, or none.** Mind Fragments uses a single trailing emoji as a
   category tag. Never mid-title.
5. **Selective caps work but read cheaper.** Infinite Wealth Lab's `RICH PEOPLE Borrow
   MONEY` style scales to 75M, but it pairs with borrowed clips. For original explainers,
   sentence case reads as authority.

## 5. The script formula, taken off the 9.8M-view Survivorship Bias

Full transcript, 18 seconds, 62 words:

> "World War II engineers mapped out the bullet holes on returning aircraft and planned to
> reinforce only those specific areas. But a statistician stepped in and pointed out a
> massive flaw. He argued that the planes that didn't return were the real evidence
> because those missing planes were likely hit in completely different areas. This is what
> we call survivorship bias."

Four beats. No greeting, no "hey guys," no subscribe CTA, no outro.

| Beat | Job | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Concrete setup** | A specific actor doing a specific thing. Starts mid-fact. | ~25 |
| 2. **The turn** | "But…" — someone spots the flaw. | ~12 |
| 3. **The reveal** | The actual mechanism, stated plainly. | ~20 |
| 4. **The name** | Name the concept. | ~7 |

Beat 4 is the one everyone skips and it's the most important. Naming the concept is what
makes the viewer feel they *learned* something rather than watched something, and it's
what drives the comment section. Never end on a CTA — end on the name.

### Duration — the number I originally got wrong

I first wrote "target 30–45 seconds" without checking the durations sitting in my own
dataset. They say something sharper. Across the 47 faceless outlier Shorts pulled:

| Slice | Duration |
|---|---|
| Top 5 by views | 18s, 14s, 18s, 22s, 24s |
| **Median of top 10** | **26s** |
| Median of all 47 | 30s |
| Under 30s / 30–60s / over 60s | 22 / 15 / 10 |

The 9.8M *Survivorship Bias* short is 62 words in 18 seconds. The 15.8M top performer is
18s; the 10.9M is 14s; an 8-second clip pulled 1.1M on a 5.6K-sub channel.

**Corrected target: 28–36 seconds, 85–110 words.** Anything past 45s is fighting the
format. All still far under the 180s Shorts cap, so none trigger the 16:9 thumbnail
requirement in `CLAUDE.md`.

### Two mechanics worth engineering deliberately

- **The loop.** Shorts reward completion over 100% — the rewatch. End on a line that
  re-frames the opening line, so the second pass lands differently.
- **The unanswered question.** Engagement in the sample ranged 0.2%–7%. The high end
  belonged to videos that left something for the viewer to finish. Close on a question
  the comments can answer, not on a summary.

## 6. Honest caveats

- **The format recommendation rests on n=1.** Under Six Minutes Studio is one channel and
  20 videos. Its 35% hit rate is the best signal in the sample and I'd still build on it,
  but it is not an established rule and I shouldn't have written it as one.
- **Days 2, 3 and 5 of the slate share a reveal shape** ("the real business isn't what you
  think"). Fine individually, repetitive in sequence — hence the spaced posting order.

### Method caveats

- **RPM coverage was 4/30.** The monetization flags are solid; the earnings picture is not.
- **Nexlev revenue estimates are AdSense guesses** and looked inflated wherever I could
  sanity-check them. I've leaned on view counts and hit rates instead, which come from
  YouTube's own listings.
- **`numOfUploads` in the Nexlev channel index is a sample count, not a real total.**
  Several channels show `ups=1` with a 353M "median" — that's one sampled video, not a
  channel with one upload. I filtered those out rather than treat them as outliers.
- **Nexlev is on the free plan**, so all `get_my_*` analytics are blocked and
  `youtube_video_details` is capped at 10 calls/24h. vidIQ credits ran out partway through,
  which cost me a second script transcript.
- **72-hour rule still applies** to anything published off the back of this.
