### A2. The patterns that drive views, and the ones that drive follows

1. **Hook, value, impact, all inside the first seconds.** The Sunnah Guy's published formula:
   - a hook in the first 3 seconds that is "simple, universally understood, and creates curiosity";
   - one focused value point;
   - an impact or conclusion line.

   He credits it with going from 1M to 2M in four months. For 15 seconds, that means the hook takes 0–2s, the single point takes 2–12s, and the line that lands takes 12–15s.
2. **Open on a human moment, not a slogan.** His biggest reel opens on a specific, painful scene (burying a father), not on "Allah is with you." What lands is a named feeling or situation. Generic comfort does not.
3. **Rawness beat polish.** He moved from cinematic edits to a raw bench format "without losing momentum." Production value is not the moat. Specificity of thought is.
4. **A recurring format with a name earns the follow.** Dr. Faraj Hasan's #ReciteWithMe makes recitation a repeatable ritual the viewer comes back for. Accounts that only post one-off quotes give no reason to follow.
5. **The faceless approach works when the visuals carry meaning.**
   - @islam pairs reminders with footage that is itself a sign: Makkah, unusual weather framed as signs of Allah.
   - @islamic_media66 reaches ~345K as a daily, faceless Quran and Sunnah page.
   - A faceless page wins on what the text says and how unusual the footage is. A pretty background alone does not.
6. **Calendar moments multiply reach.** Ramadan figures dwarf everything else. Recurring calendar anchors (Friday/Jumu'ah, before sleep, the last 10 nights) are dependable spikes. *Inference from the Ramadan figures above.*
7. **Batching enables a daily cadence.** "Four days of work, 30 days of output." A one-person faceless account can post daily only if production is templated.

### A3. Why reminder accounts stay invisible

- **The generic-quote trap.** "Allah is with you," "Trust Allah's plan" and similar lines have no hook, no source and no specific person. The small pages in rows 11–12 post this kind of content at volume and stay under 10K. *(Inference from their profile descriptions and counts. Their individual videos could not be pulled.)*
- **The same sad aesthetic.** Rain on glass, a lone person walking, a slow piano-style nasheed. It is indistinguishable from thousands of other pages, so it gives the viewer no reason to remember *this* account.
- **No source on screen.** An unsourced "hadith" invites "is this authentic?" fights in the comments and erodes trust. Fabricated "hadith" are common in this niche.
- **No series and no promise.** If the viewer can't name what they'll get tomorrow, they like and leave.
- **Music.** Many Muslim viewers object to instruments, and there is a long-running permissibility debate. A vocals-only market exists (Halal Beats) because creators need instrument-free audio. Comment-section fights over music push the conversation off-topic. **Default: voice only, or recitation.**
- **Hooks that don't resolve truthfully.** A clickbait hook that misstates religion gets corrected in the comments and burns trust. Every hook below resolves to exactly what the source says.

### A4. Platform note

TikTok's Creator Rewards programme pays only for videos **over 1 minute**, so 15-second videos earn nothing directly. *(Programme terms as I know them; check current terms.)* A 15-second account is a reach-and-follow engine. Monetisation, if it comes, will come from longer posts or elsewhere.

## C. Scripts: 20 ready-to-shoot 15-second reminders

**Production defaults for every script:**
- **Format:** 9:16 vertical.
- **Text:** white or cream on-screen text with a soft shadow, centred in the upper third, never over the bottom 20% where captions and UI sit.
- **Voiceover:** calm, unhurried, about 35 words maximum in 15 seconds.
- **Music:** none. Each script gives the audio: voice-only, recitation, or silent.
- **Source on screen:** the reference stays on screen for the whole payoff.
- **Visual signature:** each series opens with the same visual (same card style, same corner tag) so viewers recognise it in a second.
- **Recitation audio:** if you use a famous reciter's recording, check its licence. The safer route is to recite yourself or use a reciter who allows reuse.

### The four series (the follow mechanic)

| Series | Corner tag | Promise | Slot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1: One Ayah For When…** | `ONE AYAH FOR WHEN…` | Name a feeling, answer it with one exact ayah | Daily, afternoon |
| **S2: The Sunnah Nobody Posts** | `THE SUNNAH NOBODY POSTS` | A real, authenticated moment from the Prophet's ﷺ life or words that feels surprisingly human | 3 times a week |
| **S3: Before You Sleep** | `BEFORE YOU SLEEP · NIGHT #` | One authentic bedtime practice, numbered nightly | Nightly, after Isha local time |
| **S4: Small Deed, Heavy Scale** | `SMALL DEED · HEAVY SCALE` | A deed under 10 seconds long with a sahih reward | 2–3 times a week |

The follow trigger closes every video. In S1 it is "Follow — tomorrow's ayah is for [next feeling]." In S3 it is "Night 2 tomorrow." Numbering does the work.

## D. Posting plan

**Cadence:**
- 2 posts a day for the first 30 days.
- **S3 goes out nightly** after Isha in your main audience's time zone.
- One other series post goes out midday or afternoon, rotating S1, S2, S1, S4.
- Batch-produce weekly. Templates make that possible: every series has a fixed opening card.

**Best times (evidence):**
- Buffer (2026, 7.1M posts, median engagement rate): Saturday is the strongest day, Sunday 9 a.m. the best single hour, and 12–5 p.m. is generally the weakest window.
- Sprout Social (2026, ~2B engagements): Tuesday–Thursday, 2–6 p.m.
- The two studies conflict. Treat both as starting points and switch to your own TikTok analytics (Followers → activity) after week 2.
- **Islamic-calendar timing** is my inference, not measured: S3 at bedtime, and S1 Kahf or Jumu'ah-themed posts on Thursday night or Friday.

**First 30 days (order):**
- **Days 1–3:**
  - Script 1, the strongest hook.
  - Script 13, which starts the nightly numbered series.
  - Script 8, the most shareable.
- **Days 4–10:**
  - S3 Nights 2–7: Scripts 14, 15, 16, then Al-Mulk (Tirmidhi 2891), the bedtime du'a (Bukhari 6312), and a recap of Night 1.
  - Midday slot: Scripts 3, 9, 17, 2, 10, 4, 18.
- **Days 11–20:**
  - Remaining scripts 5, 6, 7, 11, 12, 19, 20, plus new scripts from the verified bank, written with Prompts 2–3.
- **Days 21–30:**
  - Double down on the series with the best follows per 1K views.
  - Remake the top 3 videos with different hooks (Prompt 2) and compare.
- **Profile:**
  - Bio names the four series and the promise ("A new ayah, sunnah and bedtime practice every day — every source cited").
  - Pin one video from each of S1, S3 and S2.

**What to measure (per video, from TikTok analytics):**
- **Follows per 1K views.** This is the primary metric, since the account exists to earn follows.
- **Average watch time vs 15s, and full-watch %.** Average watch time above 15s means viewers are rewatching, so the loop is working.
- **Shares and saves per 1K views.** S3 and S4 should lead on saves.
- **Profile visits per 1K views.** This shows whether series branding makes viewers curious about the account.
- **Benchmarks:** I found none verified for this niche. Set your own baseline from the first 10 posts.

**When to judge:**
- **Hold off before 72 hours.** Sub-1-minute TikToks often get a second push after the first test audience.
- **At 72 hours:** read retention and follows per 1K views.
- **At 7 days:** final verdict and series ranking.
- **Retire a series after 8 posts** if its follows per 1K views stays below the account average. Promote the winner to a second daily slot.
