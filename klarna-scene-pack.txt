BRAND-SAFETY NOTE — read before generating
================================================================================

No prompt in this pack names or depicts the Klarna wordmark, its pink, or any
real retailer's branding. The on-screen app is a GENERIC checkout sheet. The
title and description name the company — which is fair comment on a financial
product — but the visuals stay unbranded. Keep it that way: a generated
lookalike logo is both a trademark problem and an AI-artefact magnet.

DESCRIPTION
Four payments. Zero interest. Nothing extra to pay. So who is paying for it?

Klarna does not lend you money — it pays the shop in full, today, and then you
owe Klarna. That single fact decides everything that follows.

The shop does not receive the full price. It receives it minus a fee, often
around 3-6%. No shop absorbs that. It goes into the sticker, which means the
price rises for everyone in the shop — including the person paying cash who has
never used the app.

Three questions that settle it, whatever the brand:

1. Is this a sale, or a loan? They paid the shop; you owe them. That is a loan.
2. Does anyone profit from that loan? Not from you — from the merchant's fee. A
   loan that brings a benefit to the lender is the exact phrase scholars argue
   over.
3. Would cash have been cheaper? If later costs more than now, you paid for time.

Scholars genuinely differ. Same price as cash, no late fee, no interest — and
many permit it. Add a penalty that becomes the lender's profit, or a higher price
for paying later, and the disagreement ends.

And "buy now, pay later" was never the problem. Islam has had it for fourteen
hundred years — murabaha. The seller buys the thing, actually owns it, and sells
it to you at one disclosed price, fixed at signing, paid over time. Late? You owe
the same. Not a penny more.

One price. One owner. No penalty.
