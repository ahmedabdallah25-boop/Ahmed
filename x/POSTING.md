# Posting from your phone

## First, the honest part

**I can't guarantee any of these go viral, and nobody can.** Reach on X is decided by an
algorithm nobody outside the company can see, seeded by an audience you don't have yet.
Anyone promising virality is selling something.

What's actually true, and worth planning around:

- **Your first ~30 posts will get very little reach no matter how good they are.** A new
  account has no engagement history, so X has nothing to extrapolate from. This is not a
  verdict on the work.
- **The posts are not what breaks the cold start. The replies are.** Your diagrams are
  what people find *after* a reply makes them curious enough to tap your profile. That
  ordering matters: a great post to nobody does nothing.
- **A realistic month one is 20-50 followers who genuinely care**, and proof the template
  holds. Not a hit. If you judge month one by whether something went viral, you'll quit in
  week three — and week three is roughly where accounts that would have worked die.

What I *can* do is raise the ceiling: the diagrams are saveable, the format is
recognisable, every number is defensible, and the assumption lines mean an expert
quote-tweeting you has nothing to dunk on. That's the controllable part. The rest is
volume and time.

## The posting loop (phone, ~10 min per post)

1. **Save the image** from this chat to your camera roll.
2. **X app → compose (+) → attach the image.**
3. **Paste the caption** from the table below. One line. Never a summary of the image —
   its only job is to make someone stop and look.
4. **Add alt text.** Tap the image → "+ALT". Alt text is in the table. A screen reader
   cannot read a chart; without it, your entire account is invisible to blind users. It
   costs 15 seconds.
5. **No links in the post.** If you want to point at the YouTube video, put it in a reply
   after the post has been up an hour.
6. **Post at ~9am, second at ~7pm**, in your audience's timezone.
7. **Stay in the replies for 30 minutes.** Answer every single reply, including the rude
   ones, especially in the first hour.
8. **Then go reply to 20 other people.** This is the actual growth work. The post was the
   easy part.

## Three things that will quietly kill this

- **Posting and leaving.** A post with no author replies in the first hour is a post that
  stops.
- **Deleting weak posts.** It doesn't reset anything and it destroys your own data.
- **Breaking the template because you're bored.** You'll be sick of it around post 40.
  Your audience will only just be starting to recognise it.

## When someone corrects your numbers
They will, and on a finance account that's good — it means people with expertise are
reading. Every diagram carries its assumptions on the image, so the honest reply is
usually "yes, at those inputs — here's the one I used and why." Never delete the post,
never argue. A visible, calm correction is worth more than the original post.
