WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

The recorded voiceover is the long-form 47-scene script — 173.28s. The supplied
.mov carries that whole read but only 48.0s of picture: sixteen static stills held
a flat 3.00s each, then 125.2s of black. Those sixteen are exactly the frames in
klarnaimageprompts.txt v2, F01 through F11B, and they are good. They just cover a
55.37s cut that was never recorded.

So 125 seconds of this film has narration and no photograph. This file is the
missing picture: 31 frames, one per gap, placed on the voiceover line each belongs
to. With the existing 16 that makes 47 — one per scene of the long-form script,
which is what the scene pack always specified.

HOW THESE DIFFER FROM THE 16

The existing frames are the film's set pieces: the phone, the aisle, the basket,
the plinth. These are the connective tissue between them, and they are written to
a tighter rule:

  * ONE OBJECT, OR TWO IN RELATION. Nothing here is a scene. Each frame states a
    single fact — a slice taken off a stack, two cards that are not level, a stamp
    that has not come down yet. If a frame needs a sentence to explain it, it is
    the wrong frame.
  * THEY REUSE THE FILM'S OWN PROPS. The cream card, the banknote stack, the brass
    stamp, the brass bar, the wool jacket, the walnut and charcoal surfaces. No new
    vocabulary — a viewer should feel these were shot the same afternoon.
  * THEY LEAVE ROOM FOR TYPE. Most of these sit under kinetic typography, so the
    subject stays low and small and the top half stays dark and quiet. They are
    backgrounds that happen to be photographs, not competing hero frames.
