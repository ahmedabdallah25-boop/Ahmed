EPIDEMIC SOUND — HOW TO USE THE AUDIO TAGS
================================================================================

Every scene below carries a line like:

  🎵 mood: Dark + Suspense · genre: Cinematic / Drone · energy 2 · 60–70 BPM
     search: "sparse cinematic drone tension unresolved"

`mood:` values are taken from **Epidemic Sound's own mood vocabulary** so you can
type them straight into the mood filter rather than translating: Angry, Dark,
Dreamy, Elegant, Epic, Euphoric, Floating, Happy, Heavy & Ponderous, Hopeful,
Laid Back, Mysterious, Peaceful, Restless, Running, Sad, Scary, Sentimental,
Smooth, Sneaking, Suspense, Weird.

`energy` is 1–10 on their energy slider. `search:` is a phrase to paste into the
prompt box if you are generating rather than browsing.

**The arc matters more than any single cue.** Do not score this scene by scene with
twelve different tracks — that is the single most common way an explainer starts
feeling like a trailer. Pick **three beds** and let them run:

  BED A · Scenes 01–09    Dark, Mysterious, Suspense · energy 2–3 · unresolved
  BED B · Scenes 10–22    Sentimental, Hopeful, Floating · energy 3–5 · warming
  BED C · Scenes 23–30    Hopeful, Peaceful, Epic (restrained) · energy 4–6 · resolves

The per-scene tags then tell you **where inside each bed to sit** — where to drop
to nothing, where to let it build, where the one hit lands (Scene 27).

ONE HARD RULE ON MUSIC: cut it entirely under every recitation and under every
Arabic word study. Scenes 04, 11, 12, 19, 25 are marked `MUSIC: OUT`. A bed under
recitation is the fastest way to lose the exact viewer this channel is for.

IF YOU USE NO INSTRUMENTS: some creators in this niche don't. Every cue below works
as sound design instead — Epidemic's ambience and drone libraries cover it. Swap
`genre: Cinematic` for `genre: Ambience / Drone` and search the same mood words;
the arc is unchanged. Room tone, paper, a distant call, wind. Your call, not mine.


RUNTIME AND VISUAL DENSITY — READ BEFORE YOU GENERATE ANYTHING
================================================================================

**Two numbers in the first draft of this pack were wrong, and both mattered.**

RUNTIME. The header said ~9:00. Measured: the script is **767 words**, which at a
measured narration rate of 140 wpm is **5:28 of actual speech**. Adding the
Al-Falaq recitation (~40s), the six MUSIC: OUT silences (~12s) and inter-scene
beats (~21s) gives **6:54**. The original timecodes implied 80 wpm — nobody
speaks that slowly, so 21 of 30 scenes had been allotted more time than their
words could fill. Every timecode above has been recomputed from its own word
count and now adds up.

**Do not pad this back to 9:00.** Your best video is 6:17 and 6:54 sits right
beside it. YouTube rewards the percentage watched as much as the minutes, and a
tight 6:54 that holds 60% beats a padded 9:30 that holds 35%. If you want a
longer video later, that is a bigger argument, not more words around this one.

VISUAL DENSITY. This is the real answer to "are 30 images enough".

  30 images over 6:54  =  13.8 seconds per image

That is **not enough**, and it is not close. Engaging explainers change the frame
every 3–6 seconds; even slow, contemplative channels move every 8–10. Fourteen
seconds on one static drawing is precisely where a viewer decides they can get
this faster somewhere else — and on a 6:54 video, two or three of those decisions
is your retention graph.

The fix is NOT ninety images. Ninety generations would cost three times as much
and, worse, would drift in style across the video — the exact failure your fixed
style block exists to prevent.

**The fix is that each image is REVEALED, not cut to.** Generate every scene
twice, same prompt, same seed:

  PASS 1   append to the prompt:  line art only, no colour, no wash
  PASS 2   the prompt exactly as written in the scene

Then in the edit, cross-dissolve pass 1 into pass 2 over 1.5–2.5 seconds. The ink
appears, then the watercolour blooms into it. You are showing the drawing being
made — which is native to this aesthetic rather than imposed on it, and it is a
signature the channel can own.

That gives you the budget you need:

  30 scenes × 2 states (line → wash)           60 visual events
  ~10 typeset Arabic / on-screen text frames   10 visual events
  ------------------------------------------------------------
  ~70 events over 414 seconds                  ≈ 5.9s per event

**5.9 seconds is inside the band.** Same 30 generations doubled to 60, no style
drift, no new subjects to invent.

THREE RULES THAT COME WITH IT

1. **Every frame moves.** A slow push-in of 3–5% over the scene's full length, or
   a slow drift across the parchment grain. Never a locked-off still. This costs
   nothing in the edit and it is the difference between "illustrated" and "a
   slideshow".

2. **The reveal is timed to the sentence, not the scene.** Hold the line-art
   state while the VO sets up the idea; let the wash bloom on the word that lands.
   Scene 18 is the model: line art through "he takes the precaution", wash floods
   on "hands the outcome straight back".

3. **The longest holds need a third state.** Any scene over 20s — 11, 24 — needs
   one more event inside it: a single element arriving late, or the frame settling
   to a wider crop. Scene 11 solves itself, because the surah builds verse by
   verse on screen, which is five events on its own.

WHERE THE FRAME IS DELIBERATELY EMPTY
Scene 19 has no illustration by design — the verse alone, held silent. Scene 04
is bare paper with a single stroke. Do not "fix" these by adding imagery. They are
the rests, and a video with no rests reads as loud rather than as urgent.

STYLE BLOCK — prepend to every image prompt, verbatim
================================================================================

minimalist hand-drawn ink and watercolor sketch, textured parchment paper,
simple black line art, soft earthy watercolor washes, muted color palette,
traditional storybook illustration aesthetic, plain off-white background with
vignette color fading, 16:9

  This is the channel's established string, supplied by the owner 2026-08-17.
  **Use it exactly.** An earlier draft of this pack substituted a style of my own
  invention — "editorial illustration", a named indigo-and-deep-teal palette,
  "loose confident linework". Three of those actively fight the real one:

    · "editorial illustration" is a colder, sparser register than
      **traditional storybook**, which is warmer and narrative. Wrong register
      across 30 images is a visibly different channel.
    · indigo and deep teal are **cool**. The real palette is **soft earthy**
      washes. Naming cool hues would have pulled every frame off-brand.
    · the real string is **minimalist** with **simple black line art** and a
      **plain off-white background**. Anything specifying a room, an
      environment, or dramatic lighting contradicts it.

  So: no scene prompt below names a background, a light source, or a colour.
  The style block owns all three. The prompt names the SUBJECT only.

  The vignette does the atmospheric work. Where a scene wants a mood shift,
  it is carried by the audio bed and the edit — not by re-lighting the frame.
