## Primary

That placement is the whole argument. It takes this out of the chapter on manners and sets it down next to tawhid.

Two words carry the rest. *Birr* is expanse — goodness with room in it. Its opposite, *uquq*, is the cut. Not "disrespect." A severing. The Quran is not describing a bad mood at dinner. It is describing something torn.

The section that took longest to write is at 22:05. Almost everything said about honouring parents quietly assumes the parent was safe. The Quran does not assume that. 31:15 draws the line in a single clause — the obedience has a ceiling, and the birr does not.

So, two questions. Which of the five verses did you not know were connected? And if you are carrying the hard version of this — a parent you cannot be near, or one you do not know how to honour without being harmed — put it below. You will not be the only person in this thread carrying it.

## Why this shape

- **Opens on the fact, not a greeting.** Only the first ~2 lines show in the
  collapsed preview; "Five times the Quran names your parents" has to do all
  the work of earning the expand.
- **Gives away the second-best idea, not the best one.** The birr/uquq pair is
  worth the read on its own, so the comment is not a trailer. The five-verse
  structure and 31:15 stay pointers into the video.
- **Two questions, one easy and one hard.** The easy one ("which of the five")
  is answerable by anyone who watched and produces volume. The hard one
  produces the long replies that hold a thread open for weeks.
- **`*asterisks*` render bold on YouTube.** Left as-is deliberately.
- No subscribe ask — the description already carries one, and a second in the
  pinned slot costs more than it returns.
