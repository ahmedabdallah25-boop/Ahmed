# What a top channel actually does when the numbers stop — and Clarity's version

**Written 2026-08-24.** Reverse-engineered from live data, not from advice posts. Where a claim
comes from a blog or from YouTube's own documentation it is labelled as such; everything else is a
number pulled today.

**Channel state at the time of writing:** 44 subscribers · 18 uploads (15 long-form, 3 Shorts) ·
**2,578 lifetime views** · no banner · playlists, cross-links, trailer, shelves and three-language
localizations all live from the 17–22 August passes.

---

## The case study turned out better than expected

The plan was to study a winning channel. What the data actually shows is more useful:
**[@deepmadesimple](https://www.youtube.com/@deepmadesimple) is in a downturn right now**, and
that is exactly the condition this question is about.

Its lifetime average is **71,889 views per video**. Its last thirty uploads (3–24 August, pulled
today):

| | |
|---|---:|
| Uploads in the window | **30 in 21 days** |
| Median views | **9,250** |
| Below the channel's own lifetime average | **29 of 30** |
| The exception | *What "Honor Your Parents" Actually Means in Hebrew* — 345,000 |

So a 258,000-subscriber channel is currently running at roughly an eighth of its own average, on
the same format, with one video in thirty breaking out. Everything below is what it does *in that
condition* — not what it did while winning.

That matters for a second reason. Clarity's twin of that 345K video — same title, "Arabic" for
"Hebrew" — has **24 views**. The gap is not a mystery of taste. It is seven mechanical differences,
and six of them are free.

# The seven moves

## 1. Clone the winner within 48 hours, not 90 days

**The evidence.** *Honor Your Parents* landed on 10 August. What the reference channel published
next:

| Date | Title | Views |
|---|---|---:|
| Aug 10 | What "Honor Your Parents" Actually Means in **Hebrew** | **345K** |
| Aug 12 | What "Be Still" Actually Means in **Hebrew** | 66K |
| Aug 14 | What "All Things Work Together for Good" Actually Means in **Greek** | 22K |
| Aug 17 | What Does **Ruach** Mean in **Hebrew**? Wind, Breath, Spirit | 6.2K |
| Aug 18 | What "Came to Himself" Really Means in Luke 15:17 | 7.4K |
| Aug 19 | The **Hebrew** Word for Bless Is the Word for Knee | 9.6K |
| Aug 24 | What **Kairos and Chronos** Actually Mean in the Bible | 3.5K |

Six re-runs of one title frame in fourteen days. Note that the clones did **not** repeat the hit —
they returned 3.5K–66K against 345K. They ran it anyway. The frame is cheap to run and the payoff
is asymmetric: one more 345K pays for twenty 6K's.

**The mechanism.** A breakout tells you a specific audience exists and YouTube has found a door to
it. Cloning the frame while that audience is still warm asks YouTube to walk through the same door
again. Ninety days later the door is a different door.

**Clarity's version — and this is the largest missed move on the channel.**

Clarity's best video is *What the Quran Actually Says About Anger*, 849 views, **a third of the
channel's lifetime audience**. It won on 9 May. The frame has not been run once in the 107 days
since.

Worse, look at when its siblings shipped:

> Anxiety (126) was published 10 May. Depression (43) on 11 May. Promises (16) on 12 May.
> **All of them went out before the Anger result was known.** The channel cloned its winner
> in advance, by accident, into a ten-video dump that split the test ten ways — and then never
> cloned it again once it knew which one had won.

That is the difference in one sentence: **the reference clones in response to evidence; Clarity
cloned before there was any.**

Both of Clarity's two proven title frames are the reference channel's two proven title frames:

| Frame | Clarity's proof | The reference's proof |
|---|---|---|
| `[universal problem] + what the [text] actually says` | Anger — **849** | Anxiety — 215K |
| `[one word] + the meaning you were not taught` | Money in the Quran: The Arabic Word That Changes Everything — **226** | Honor Your Parents / Hesed / Ruach — 345K / 143K / 6K |

The slate in §"Clarity's version, in order" below merges the two.

## 2. Keep one anchor series pinned to demand that recurs on a calendar

**The evidence.** Four of the reference's last thirty uploads are a numbered weekly series:
`✅Sabbath School Lesson 06…09 | 1 and 2 Corinthians (Q3 2026)`.

| | Anchor series | Everything else |
|---|---:|---:|
| Count in the window | 4 | 26 |
| **Median views** | **33,500** | 8,850 |
| Worst result | 8,700 | 818 |

The anchor series is **3.8× the median of everything else** on the same channel in the same three
weeks. It is not creative — it is a numbered lesson on a fixed weekly syllabus that millions of
people study on a schedule. It does not depend on the algorithm being in a good mood, because the
demand arrives by the calendar.

**This niche has the same mechanism, and it is bigger.** Muslims are taught to read **Surah
Al-Kahf every Friday**. vidIQ's daily search-volume series for `surah al kahf`, 27 July – 23 August:

| Day | Median searches/hour |
|---|---:|
| Monday | 20,790 |
| Tuesday | 18,499 |
| Wednesday | 20,779 |
| **Thursday** | **38,496** |
| **Friday** | **301,999** |
| Saturday | 31,263 |
| Sunday | 19,117 |

**Friday runs at 13.4× the non-Friday median, four Fridays out of four, no exceptions.** Thursday
is already at 1.9× — the demand starts building the day before. The term itself is 190,340
searches/month (`surah kahf`, 978,793), top markets PK 25%, BD 22%, MA 12%, IN 10%, GB 5%.

**The honest caveat, stated before the plan and not after it.** That spike is the whole query
family, and most of it is *recitation* intent — people looking for audio to listen to on Friday,
which is not what this channel makes. The explainer slice is much smaller. But it is measurable and
it is still enormous relative to Clarity: *The Hidden Meanings in Surah Al-Kahf That Most Muslims
Miss* (Quranic Tales, 21:31, faceless) has **34,785 views**, and a 53:57 Al-Kahf explainer posted
one month ago has **68,872**. Clarity's entire lifetime is 2,578.

**Clarity's version:** a six-episode Al-Kahf series, **published Thursday** so it is indexed and in
the feed before the Friday wave. Detail in §"Clarity's version, in order".

## 3. Put the entity in the title

**The evidence.** Thirteen of the reference channel's last thirty titles name a book, a chapter or
a verse — *Luke 15:17*, *Mark 4*, *Genesis 3*, *Genesis 28*, *Jeremiah 29:11*, *John 1:1*, *1 and 2
Corinthians*, *Ruth*, *Ephesians*. Roughly half.

**Clarity: one of fifteen.** Only `5Fb1iERyIhs` names a surah, and only because it was retitled by
hand on 22 August.

**Why it matters more here than there.** This niche's search demand is organised *by surah name* to
a degree the Bible niche is not: `surah al kahf` 190K/mo, `surah kahf` 978K/mo, and a plain
"Surah 1: Al-Fatihah… explained in English" has 583,640 views. A title that says "What the Quran
Says About Anger" matches no entity at all; a title that says "Surah Ash-Shura 37" matches a thing
people type.

**Clarity's version.** Every *new* title names the surah in plain ASCII, with the numeric citation
in the description's first two lines. This applies going forward only — see move 6 for why the
back catalogue is left alone — with one exception noted in "Applied this pass": the surah names now
go into the channel keywords, which is a channel-level surface and costs nothing.

## 4. Lock every variable except the one being tested

**The evidence.** The twenty-six non-anchor uploads in the last three weeks:

> **Runtime range 25:00 – 28:35. Median 26:54. Twenty-six of twenty-six inside a four-minute band.**

That is not a coincidence of subject matter. It is a locked format. When a channel's output varies
only in topic, every result is readable as a topic result.

**Clarity has changed runtime on three consecutive uploads:** 10:14 (16 Aug), 31:29 (19 Aug),
18:08 (22 Aug) — and retitled the third one fourteen hours after publishing it. Three uploads, four
moving variables, no readable result among them.

**Clarity's version:** pick a runtime and hold it for six uploads. The evidence says pick **22–28
minutes** (move 7 of `video-formula.md` argues this at length and it has not changed). Then change
nothing else but the topic.

## 5. Publish into the downturn — do not pause

**The evidence.** Thirty uploads in twenty-one days, *while* running at an eighth of the channel
average. The response to the drop was not a pause, a re-strategy, or a "we're taking a break"
video. It was volume, at an unchanged format.

**The honest confound, unchanged from `video-formula.md` §7.** I cannot separate "the format works"
from "the format was run 193 times". Nobody can, without the retention data both channels have
paywalled. What the data does support is the negative claim, and it is the one that matters here:
**stopping is what killed Clarity's first run.** Ten videos in nine days, then a 55-day blackout
across exactly the window when the 849-view result was worth compounding.

**Clarity's version:** two long-form per week — Thursday (anchor) and Sunday (slate) — and never
zero. Not 1.1/day; that is not achievable here and pretending otherwise is how the last plan broke.

## 6. Do not repackage the settled catalogue. This is the counter-intuitive one.

Everything written about "reviving dead videos" says change the title and thumbnail. **On this
channel that is wrong, and there is a measurement.**

- **YouTube's own mechanic:** changing a title or thumbnail does not itself trigger re-promotion.
  The algorithm responds to *viewer behaviour on impressions*, not to the edit. No impressions, no
  behaviour, no change. ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-6-questions-answered/389181/),
  [Grupa BB Media](https://grupabbmedia.com/blog/does-the-change-of-metadata-restart-the-youtube-algorithm))
- **The refresh literature agrees on the condition:** repackage where a video has *impressions and
  a weak click-rate*. That is a CTR problem. ([Overseeros](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-content-refresh-old-video-strategy))
- **This channel measured it directly.** Retitling a settled video on channel 1 returned **+0 views
  over 21.7 hours**. Clarity's May uploads are at 0.2–2.1 views/day. There is no impression stream
  to convert.

**What the same evidence *does* license — and it is not the same thing.** YouTube's **Test &
Compare** (Studio → the video → Test & Compare) A/B tests up to three thumbnails, and since 2026
titles too, splitting real traffic for up to 14 days. Note what decides it: **watch time per
impression, not raw click-rate** — so it cannot be won with a thumbnail that oversells.
([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en-GB),
[Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-test-compare/))

That is a tool for a video that is *currently being shown to people*. Which on this channel means
**the next upload, in its first two weeks** — never the May catalogue.

**Clarity's version:**

- **Do not touch** the four protected winners or the eleven other settled uploads. `packaging-fix.json`
  already refuses their ids.
- **Do** run Test & Compare on every new upload once it clears ~1,000 impressions. It is Studio-only
  — there is no Data API for it — so it is a manual step, and it belongs in the upload checklist.
- The back catalogue's remaining unclaimed value is **not** in its packaging. It is in **chapters**
  (3 of 15 have them; they generate "key moments" in search) and **captions** (`hasCaption: false`
  on all 18, and captions are what YouTube auto-translates for the Arabic, Urdu and Bengali
  metadata that went live on 22 August). Both are production work, not metadata writes.

## 7. One spine, so a single winner irrigates everything else

**The evidence.** The reference channel runs a **single 71-video playlist** — "The Bible EXPLAINED"
— rather than themed lists, has a **Home tab** with sections, and puts an "IN THIS VIDEO" contents
block plus a 400-word "RELATED SEARCHES" paragraph and exactly five hashtags in every description.
It has **no banner** at 258K subscribers, and it is filed under **People & Blogs**, not Education.

**Clarity's version is largely already built** — four playlists, 15 of 15 long-form in one,
WATCH NEXT blocks on every description, trailer set to the 849-view Anger video, four section
shelves. That work was done on 17 and 22 August and it does not need redoing.

Two notes from the comparison:

- **The banner is confirmed cosmetic.** Third pass demoted it; the reference channel still has none.
  It stays demoted.
- **Category.** The reference is *People & Blogs*; Clarity is *Education*. I have no evidence that
  this is load-bearing and am not recommending a change — recording it only so the next pass does
  not "discover" it as a finding.

# What pushing is *not*

Named so they stop coming up:

| Not a lever | Why |
|---|---|
| Retitling settled videos | +0 measured; no impressions to convert (move 6) |
| Deleting or unlisting underperformers | Removes indexed surface. YouTube does not reward pruning |
| A channel banner | Reference channel has none at 258K subs |
| More hashtags | Over fifteen and YouTube ignores **all** of them — this channel already shipped that bug once, on `5Fb1iERyIhs` |
| Posting the video in Islamic groups / comment sections | Traffic that does not watch damages the signal it is meant to send, and it will get the channel reported |
| Another ten-video burst | It is the documented cause of the May collapse |
| Changing country to chase RPM | Dishonest packaging on a channel whose proposition is honesty |

### C. Build spec deltas — what changes versus `video-formula.md`

The existing spec stands. Three amendments from today's data:

1. **Runtime 22–28 minutes, held for six uploads.** The reference's own current band is 25:00–28:35
   across 26 of 26. `video-formula.md` said 19–28; today's data narrows it and, more importantly,

2. **The surah name goes in the title, in ASCII.** New in this pass (move 3).
3. **Test & Compare on day 3–14 of every upload.** New in this pass (move 6). Studio-only; no

### D. Applied this pass

Written to `clarity/packaging-fix.json`, applied by **"CLARITY IN THE QURAN - 2. Fix packaging"**:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Channel keywords | 33 terms / 258 chars | **26 terms / 473 chars**, incl. seven surah names |
| Channel description | @deepmadesimple's About with the nouns swapped | rewritten — 931 chars |

Fewer keyword *terms*, nearly twice the characters: the old list was single words split by the
API's own tokenisation (`"verse"`, `"by"`, `"verse"`), so the count was never 33 real terms. The
new list is 26 whole phrases, and it is the first time this channel's keywords contain a surah name.

**On the description.** The old text was the source channel's About with "Bible"→"Quran" — the same
substitution that produced *"The cross is not the answer here"*, removed from `cjtKWsFZbcg` on 17
August. The standing rule after that leak is to audit for the register rather than for the one line
already found, and this is the largest derived asset nobody had re-read. It also promised "New
videos every week", which the channel has not done since May. The replacement states the format
being run, names five surahs, keeps the verified-mushaf and no-ruling notes, and keeps the one line
of the original that is doing real work — the "I have no idea what they're talking about" close.

`clarity_packaging.py` now supports a channel description, **guarded**: it must still contain the
fragment it expects, or the write is skipped rather than overwriting a Studio edit nobody recorded
here. Same discipline as `expect_current_title` on `set_packaging`.

### E. Not done, and why

- **Chapters** on the 12 uploads without them — needs someone who has watched each video. Largest
  remaining free surface.
- **Captions** — `hasCaption: false` on all 18, and now worth more than before, because captions
  are what YouTube auto-translates for the three localized languages already live.
- **Thumbnails on the back catalogue** — still never seen. `i.ytimg.com` is blocked at this
  gateway (re-tested today, connection refused), so no pass has been able to look at them. Per move
  6 this is *not* urgent for settled videos; it is urgent for anything published from here.
- **Test & Compare** — Studio-only, manual, on the owner.
- **A native Bengali reader** — unchanged, and the only open item with a credibility cost.

# What would falsify this

Stated in advance so the next pass cannot rationalise the result.

1. **The anchor.** If Al-Kahf E1 and E2, published Thursday, do not beat the channel's non-anchor
   median views/day at 7 days, the Friday-demand thesis is wrong for the *explainer* slice and the
   series should be cut to the four story episodes and shipped whenever.
2. **The clone.** If two slate videos at 22–28 minutes both land under the 849 of the Anger video
   at 30 days, then the winner was the topic, not the frame, and the frame is not worth cloning.
3. **The runtime.** Six uploads held inside 22–28 minutes with everything else steady is the first
   readable runtime test this channel will have run. If their median views/day does not beat the
   6–11 minute catalogue's, the long-form thesis inherited from the reference channel does not
   transpose to a 44-subscriber channel and should be dropped rather than defended.

And the standing rule that outranks all three: **nothing is judged before 72 hours.** The 22 August
upload is currently at 16.0 views/day, the best rate of any long-form on this channel including the
849 — and it is two days old, which under this channel's own rule is not a verdict.
