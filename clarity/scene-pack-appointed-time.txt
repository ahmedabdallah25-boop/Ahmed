READ THE SEVEN HARD RULES BEFORE YOU GENERATE ANYTHING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. NO FACES. NO PROPHETS. NO COMPANIONS. Not stylised, not distant, not in
   silhouette-with-features, not "just the outline". Nothing in this pack depicts
   the Prophet ﷺ, any prophet, or any named Companion, and no prompt below asks
   for a human face of any kind. Where a person is unavoidable the pack uses
   hands, feet, a shadow on a wall, a figure at a distance so small it is a mark,
   or the space a person has just left. Yusuf occupies four minutes of this
   script and appears in none of these frames — the well, the ledger, the cell
   door and the storehouse do the work instead.

2. NO PHOTOREAL ANYTHING. Ink and watercolour throughout. thumbnail-system.md §3
   established why: this channel carries a public "AI BE AWARE" comment, and
   photoreal AI is the signature that comment points at.

3. NO GENERATED TEXT INSIDE A GENERATED IMAGE. Image models produce nonsense
   Arabic and misspelt English, and on a Quran channel a garbled ayah on screen
   is unrecoverable. Every glyph the viewer sees — Arabic, English, numerals,
   labels — is TYPESET IN THE EDIT over a plate generated empty. Every prompt
   below therefore carries the negative block, which forbids text.

4. THE ARABIC IS SET FROM A VERIFIED MUSHAF. Copy-paste from a checked digital
   text, then have a reader confirm it on screen before render. Never retype it.

5. A FRAME CARRIES ONLY WHAT A VIEWER SHOULD READ. No production notes, no
   directions to the editor, no commentary about what the frame is doing.
   Directions live HERE, where a person reads them.

6. ARABIC SITS ABOVE. On any frame carrying both, the Arabic goes above its
   citation and above its gloss or list — never underneath. Citations use SURAH
   + NUMBER: "AL-A'RAF 34", not "AL-A'RAF · THE HEIGHTS".

7. THE RHYME RULE — THE ONE THIS PACK EXISTS FOR.
   Every scene below is headed with the exact line of voiceover it sits under.
   The image must be THAT SENTENCE, not the mood of the paragraph around it.

   The test: mute the video. A viewer who cannot hear a word should still be
   able to follow the argument off the frames alone. If a frame would work
   equally well under a different sentence in this script, it is decoration and
   it is wrong — replace it or cut it.

   124 of the 130 scenes below are headed with their line. The remaining six
   are the recitation plates, which anchor to an ayah citation instead — the
   reciter is the audio, so there is no narration line for them to rhyme with.

   This is the finding in video-formula.md §5 stated as a build rule. The
   reference channel's images are information graphics that carry the argument;
   Clarity's have been mood illustrations that decorate it. The difference is
   not prettiness. It is whether the picture is making the point.

   Worked example, from this pack. The script says the popular story is that
   "ajal is death and waqt is prayer times — two neat boxes", and then shows
   neither word will stay in its box.
     WRONG:  a mosque at dusk with a crescent moon. Islamic, atmospheric, and
             it says nothing. It would fit under any sentence in any video.
     RIGHT:  S070 draws the two boxes neatly and lets them look correct for
             four seconds. Then S071 drops the four watercolour objects from
             the previous ninety seconds — the debt tablet, the folded
             garment, the notched stone, the horizon peg — into the left box
             one at a time, and they visibly overflow it and break its
             outline. The box does not resize. It fails.
   The second one IS the argument. That is the standard for all 130 frames.

HOW 130 SCENES BECOME 280 VISUAL EVENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Counted, not estimated — the numbers below are produced by walking this file.

     64 GRAPHIC frames, built in the edit             =  64
     60 ILLUS plates, generated                       = 210
      6 RECITATION frames                             =   6
     -------------------------------------------------------
    130 scenes  →  280 events over 1,656s             = 5.9s average

Each ILLUS scene yields up to FOUR events from ONE generation:

  1. LINE     — same prompt + "line art only, no colour, no wash", same seed
  2. WASH     — the prompt exactly as written
  3. PUSH     — the wash, slow 4% push-in
  4. DRIFT    — the wash, slow lateral drift across the grain

  Events 1→2 cross-dissolve over 1.5–2.5s: the ink appears, then the colour
  blooms into it. Hold LINE while the VO sets the idea up; let the WASH bloom
  on the word that lands. In ACT 0 and ACT 7 only LINE→WASH is used — two
  events, no push, no drift — which is what makes those two stretches feel
  faster without generating a single extra plate. That is why 60 plates yield
  210 events rather than 240.

5.9s sits inside the 3–6s band video-formula.md measured on the reference
channel, and only 60 generations are needed to get there.

GRAPHIC frames are NOT generated. They are built in the edit from typeset text
on the navy ground — same palette as the thumbnails: navy #16203C, gold #E8A33D,
cream #F2ECDC, rust #C0492B. Amiri for Arabic, Archivo for Latin. These are the
frames that carry the argument, and per video-formula.md §5 they are the reason
this video survives twenty-seven minutes without a face on screen.

**18 of the 64 are information graphics** in the spec's own five categories,
listed below. That is one every 92 seconds, against the spec's budget of roughly
one every 90 — met, not exceeded, and not by rounding.

The other 46 are word plates, citation plates and reversal frames: a gold Arabic
word with its gloss, a struck reading replaced by a right one, an ayah with two
words lifted out of it. They are cheaper than an information graphic and they do
less, but on a vocabulary video they are not decoration either — a viewer who
mutes this video can still read every word the argument turns on.

  The five required graphic types, and where each is discharged:
    BIG NUMBER          S001 (٤) · S004 (٣٠٩) · S007 (١٠٠) · S095 (٤ again)
    LABELLED COMPARISON S011 (the four wrong answers) · S023 (ajal / waqt) ·
                        S054 (309 vs bid') · S105 (kept vs taken) ·
                        S112 (the two positions) · S114 (solar / lunar)
    CHAIN DIAGRAM       S026 (أ-ج-ل → four derived forms) ·
                        S061 (household → prison → kingdom) ·
                        S091 (أ-ن-ي → yaʾni, ānin, ināʾ)
    TIMELINE            S041 (the four leases) · S073 (Iblis's reprieve) ·
                        S129 (the two terms of Al-An'am 2)
    LABELLED OBJECT     S034 (the debt tablet) · S058 (the storehouse)

  And nine frames exist only to reverse something the video has already put on
  screen, which is the shape of the whole channel's thesis rendered as an edit:
    S041 strikes S031 · S071 breaks S070 · S076 corrects S023 ·
    S094 replaces S090 · S100, S105, S110, S111, S124.

RECITATION marks a place where the TTS stops and a real reciter is heard. Six of
them, 98 seconds total. Never synthesise these. Their frames render to
media/clarity/frames/_pending/ as PARTIAL FRAMES: the citation in its final
position, the ayah area left empty. No label, no dashed box, no "awaiting"
caption — the missing text is visible on its own and a note explaining itself is
how production language ends up on screen.
