## What his flow actually is

These patterns come from the transcripts of his four most-viewed YouTube Shorts, pulled on 2026-11-23:

| Video | Views |
|---|---|
| "The most beautiful practice" (`_v85a7aMwFw`) | 1.1M |
| "This will give your face beauty" (`vkH7fNlXIDQ`) | 461K |
| "When things are tight Allah says spend" (`76eAb2TMW3s`) | 400K |
| "Rizq does not follow logic" (`LQ78YLXcGV8`) | 332K |

1. **He starts mid-conversation.** He opens on a claim, not a greeting. "By the way, do you know what this iPhone is selling for in Gaza?" "Even Allah tells us, he says when you're tight, spend." The viewer feels they walked into a talk that had already started.
2. **The double-take.** He says the surprising thing, then repeats it back to himself as a question: "When things are tight… then you… huh? When it's tight, *spend*?" That echo is the hook. It gives the viewer the reaction before they have time to have their own.
3. **One concrete picture.** A price ($12,000), a number (1,000 sold), an object (Musa's staff, the snake), a time (10 minutes in sujud). Never an abstraction on its own.
4. **"Because…" and then the turn.** He reframes what the eye sees against what Allah controls: "It's not what you perceive with your eyes… it is Allah that's controlling everything."
5. **He drops one Arabic word, then explains it.** "Hold that, in Arabic, it's a command." "Rizq is a matter that's really *ajeeb*." The word does the work of authority without a lecture.
6. **Short fragments and real pauses.** "How? Don't ask me." "Two rakas. I don't want a lot." He leaves silence after the line that matters.
7. **He lands soft, with no call to action.** He ends on a principle ("then it's complete submission") or on intimacy with Allah ("Just be with Him… He sees me"). He never says "follow."

**What the old scripts did differently:** they were written as cards, with each line a separate caption, a separate idea and a separate cut. His videos are one continuous breath. The rewrite below makes the voiceover a single monologue and lets the screen follow it.

## Production changes from the original pack

- **Voiceover is the spine.** Write it, record it in one take, then cut picture to it. Do not record line by line.
- **Delivery:** close mic and a talking voice, not a reading voice. Let the double-take sound genuinely surprised. Take a full beat of silence before the quote and after the landing line.
- **On-screen text becomes word-synced captions of the VO.** One word per script is the **emphasis word**: it appears large and alone when spoken (marked **bold** below). The series corner tag stays, as before.
- **The follow trigger moves off the voiceover.** It now appears as on-screen text in the last second only, and in the caption. His flow ends quiet, and a spoken "Follow for…" breaks it. Your series numbering ("Night 2 tomorrow") still does the follow work.
- **Recitation is reduced to one Arabic word or phrase per script** (device 5). The full English quote is read in the VO. If you want recitation, put it under the final 2 seconds as the loop bed.
- **Pace:** his natural pace is about 2.5–3 words a second with pauses. Fifteen seconds holds about 35–42 words. Scripts marked **⏱ tight** run 42–50 words. Either let them run to 17–18s or cut the line in [brackets].
- **Do not imitate him.** Do not use his name in captions or tags, his catchphrases ("I'm going to decode this piece"), or an AI clone of his voice. The goal is his rhythm, not his identity. Passing yourself off as him would also get the account reported.
- **Quotes are unchanged.** Every Quran and hadith quote below is word for word from the original pack, with Sahih International and sunnah.com wording. All ⚠ SCHOLAR CHECK flags from the original still apply. New flags added here are marked ⚠ NEW.

Each script is laid out as follows: the time, the VO line (what the viewer hears), the **emphasis word** (what appears big on screen), and the picture.

## Before you record

- **Read it aloud with a stopwatch.** If a script needs more than 15 seconds at a pace that still sounds like talking, cut the [bracketed] line before you speed up. His flow depends on the pauses, and rushing kills it faster than an extra 2 seconds does.
- **Test his flow against the original card style.** Run both versions of Script 1 a week apart and compare follows per 1K views and average watch time, using the measurement plan in section D of the original pack. His clips are 35–90 seconds long, so how much of his style survives at 15 seconds is still unproven. If the monologue wins, the next test is 25–30 seconds, which is closer to where his clips actually perform.
