## 1. What it actually is

A **silent escalation ladder**. One transaction — *buy a thing, hand over money* — repeated six
times. Exactly one variable changes each time: the country, and therefore the physical volume of
cash required. Nothing else moves. Same actor, same shirt and tie, same handheld camera language,
same beat.

| # | Panel | Buys | Pays with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🇺🇸 USA | luxury car | one banknote |
| 2 | 🇰🇪 Kenya | a TV | a small stack |
| 3 | 🇳🇬 Nigeria | an iPhone | a sack, dumped on the counter |
| 4 | 🇺🇬 Uganda | a bowl of food | a stack so big they are still counting at 3:28 AM |
| 5 | 🇸🇱 Sierra Leone | a tuk-tuk fare | a flatbed truck of rice sacks full of cash |
| 6 | 🇿🇼 Zimbabwe | **four tomatoes** | an entire apartment that avalanches out the door |

The ladder inverts on purpose: **as the money gets bigger, the thing you buy gets smaller.** Panel 1
is a supercar for one note. Panel 6 is four tomatoes for a room. That inversion is the whole
engine — it is not "money is funny", it is a curve the viewer can feel bending under them.

## 2. The mechanics worth stealing

**Non-verbal.** Zero dialogue. Grunts, laughs, foley. This is why it did 12M and not 400K — it
carries across every language on the platform with no translation and no reading load. On-screen
text is *labels only* (country + flag), never explanation.

**ASL ~1.85s, ~40 cuts in 74s.** Nothing is allowed to breathe. Every shot is one action, cut on
the action, no settle frames.

**Three shots per panel, always the same three.** Approach → the money moment (close-up on hands)
→ the payoff/reaction. The repetition is what makes the escalation legible; you stop watching
*what happens* and start watching *how much worse it gets*.

**The time-card gag is the peak, and it is placed at panel 4 of 6** — not at the end. Three cards
in a row ("two hours later", "six hours later", "3:28 AM") over the same two men still counting.
It is the only moment where the edit stops advancing the plot and just twists the knife. Then it
resumes and escalates twice more.

**Hook = a supercar bought with one dollar bill, inside 3 seconds.** No setup, no title, no face
to the camera. The absurdity *is* the hook, and it is a visual you cannot resolve without
watching the next shot.

**Comment-bait is structural, not asked for.** Six countries named means every viewer from a
seventh country has to say so. He never asks for a comment; the format extracts them.

## 3. Two things NOT to copy

- **The SpongeBob title cards and the narrator voice are copyrighted.** Kennar is a
  12M-view entertainment channel absorbing that risk at scale. On a small monetised channel it is
  a live claim/strike surface for a gag you can rebuild yourself in an afternoon. Build an
  original card — see the pack below.
- **The escalation lands on real, named, currently-struggling countries.** It works for a Kenyan
  comedian speaking about his own region. Aimed outward from a UK finance channel it reads as
  punching down, and the comment section will say so. **Move the variable off nationality.**

## 4. Counsel for Finance % Decoded

**This format is a genuinely new lane for you, and that matters more than the topic.**
`channel-reset.md` says every subject family here collapses by entry 3 or 4, and the
formula-perfect third inflation video did 43 views. The problem was never the explaining — it was
that every video was *the same video*. This is a different video: no VO script, no host, no
teaching voice, no diagram. It is a joke with a number in it.

**Three real tensions, stated plainly:**

1. **It breaks `house-style.md`.** Flat 2D vector cannot sell physical weight, and weight is the
   entire joke — the sag of the sack, the door that will not close. Seedance is at its best on
   photoreal motion and at its worst on flat-vector consistency. My recommendation: run this as a
   **separate format lane**, photoreal, and keep it faceless by framing (chin-down, over-shoulder,
   hands-only). You keep the faceless promise and lose nothing that was working.
2. **It breaks the `Is [named thing] Halal?` title formula** that `next-slate.md` shows is the
   proven format in this niche. Accept that — you are testing format, not title. Don't change both.
3. **The payoff has to be a reveal, not just a laugh.** Kennar's ladder ends in absurdity. Yours
   should escalate to absurdity and then **reset**, and the reset is the Islamic-finance point.
   That final panel is the only reason this is your video and not a generic money meme.

**Hold constant:** silent, ~1.85s ASL, three shots per panel, labels-only text, one variable.

## 5. Six concepts to choose from

Ranked by how well each preserves Kennar's engine (inversion + escalation + reset).

| # | Concept | The constant | The variable that escalates | Punchline / reset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** ⭐ | **The same car, six ways to pay** | one £20,000 car | what the finance costs you | last panel: same car, same price, no interest |
| **2** | **Missing one payment, six ways** | one missed £10 payment | what £10 becomes | one system where £10 stays £10 |
| **3** | **£5 coffee, six ways to pay** | a flat white | the paperwork behind it | BNPL a coffee → a 12-page contract for £5 |
| **4** | **What a £200,000 house actually costs** | the same house | the total repaid over 25 years | the sign says £200k; the ladder says £340k |
| **5** | **Borrowing £1,000 in six systems** | £1,000, need it today | volume of cash returned | payday panel = truck; reset = envelope |
| **6** | **Insurance: six ways to be covered** | one dented car | what the policy actually pays | the pooled model, no premium kept |

**Take #1.** It has the cleanest inversion (money grows, what you get stays identical), the object
is physical and filmable, "buying a car" is universal, and the reset is the strongest halal beat
you own. #2 is the best backup and is cheaper to shoot. #4 has the biggest number but the weakest
visual — a house does not escalate on screen.

## What moves, and what is held constant

Held constant across all six panels: the car (same model, same colour, same forecourt), the buyer

The single variable under test: **the payment method, and the physical volume of money it costs.**

## Brand safety

Cars are **unbadged** — no grille logo, no wordmark, no recognisable silhouette of a current
model. Money is **generic unbranded paper notes**: plain rectangles, abstract guilloche pattern,
no numerals large enough to read, no national symbols. No real lender, dealer or card network is
named anywhere in prompt, caption or title. The subject is the *arithmetic*, which is fair comment;
the moment it is a named company it is a legal problem and a slate that dates in a month.

## The original time card (replaces the SpongeBob card)

Build in your editor, not in Seedance. Full-screen hold, 0.7s, hard cut in and out:
- Ground: flat cream `#F7EFDD`, no texture, no border flourish.
- Type: heavy condensed sans, dark brown `#3D2B23`, centred, all caps, ~14% of frame height.
- Audio: single soft marimba/woodblock ding. **No voiceover.** Silence under the card sells it.

## Caption burn-in spec

- Position: centred, lower third, ~22% up from the bottom edge. Never over a hand or a note.
- Type: heavy condensed sans, **white with a 6px hard black outline**, all caps, ~7% frame height.
- The money figure appears **on the cut**, not animated in, and holds until the panel ends.
- Panel label appears on the panel's first frame; the figure appears on the panel's *last* shot.
  Label first, number last — the number is the payoff of each panel, so make the viewer wait 2s.
- Time cards: separate full-screen graphic, spec in section above. Never overlap footage.

## Audio

No voiceover. This is not negotiable — the moment there is a narrator it becomes another
explainer and loses the entire reason it travels.
- Bed: one dry upbeat percussion loop, 100–110bpm, no melody, ducked under nothing.
- Foley on every money moment: paper riffle, zip, thud of the bag, the shoebox lid.
- **Silence under all three time cards.** The music stops dead and restarts on the cut back.
- Music drops out entirely for S21 (the side-by-side) and returns for S22.

## Retention checkpoints

- **0:00–0:03** — one note, one supercar-priced object. If this shot is soft, regenerate it.
- **0:11** — the second panel must land before 12s or the pattern never establishes.
- **0:43** — first time card. This is where a viewer decides to finish.
- **0:57** — the side-by-side. The single most screenshottable frame; make it clean.

## Accuracy notes

Every figure is **illustrative, on a £20,000 balance**, and should be captioned as a total-repaid
figure, not a headline rate. They are defensible at the order of magnitude, which is what the
video claims:

- £23,800 — 7% APR over 5 years is a mainstream personal-loan rate; total is ~£23.8k.
- £26,400 — ~10.9% APR dealer finance over 5 years lands near £26k.
- £38,000 — weekly-payment / buy-here-pay-here lots routinely total 1.8–2× the sticker.
- £46,000 / 26 years — a £20,000 balance at ~24.9% APR serviced at a 1%+interest minimum runs
  roughly a quarter-century and roughly £46k repaid. This is the one to double-check against a
  current minimum-payment calculator before publishing, because it is the headline number.
- Panel 6 claims only that the price does not change and no interest is charged. It does **not**
  claim the arrangement is free, faster, or universally available. Do not let the caption imply it.
