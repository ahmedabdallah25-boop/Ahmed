STYLE LOCK — the grammar every prompt below is written in
================================================================================

- Flat 2D vector illustration. Clean, EVEN-WEIGHT dark brown outlines on every
  shape — the line never thickens or tapers.
- Flat colour fills with only soft, minimal shading. No gradients, no glow.
- Warm cream background, approximately #F7EFDD, kept plain and uncluttered.
- Figures sit on a soft pale elliptical ground shadow. Nothing else beneath them.
- Faces are simple: solid dot eyes, fine curved eyebrows, a small simple mouth,
  a minimal nose line. Beards are flat shapes, not rendered hair.
- Rounded, friendly proportions. Characters read at roughly 6 to 7 heads tall.
- Props and environments are drawn in the SAME flat vector language as the
  characters — never photographic, never 3D.
- 9:16 vertical composition. Subject in the lower two-thirds, clean headroom
  above for burned-in captions.

TAG NOTES — retagged 2026-08-09. Not one word of the script changed; only the
tags moved. See pension-script-audit.md for the reasoning behind each one.

WHAT WAS WRONG. The first pass used 13 tags, EIGHT of them [serious]. A tag
repeated eight times stops being direction and becomes the baseline, so v3 had
one register for the whole script. That flat-serious texture is the "AI narrator" sound
that gets swiped. Three of the script's four biggest lines carried no tag at all.

THE REGISTER SYSTEM. Four registers, deliberately reused so the delivery reads as
a system rather than as random colour. This mirrors the pack's own visual grammar
(house-style.md): named people have faces and colour, the institution has neither.

  MACHINE   [flat] [matter-of-fact]  — used when the voice describes what the
            system does. The voice goes featureless where the clerk does.
  CONFIDING [quietly]                — used when speaking to the viewer as a
            person. This is the register that buys attention, not volume.
  WEIGHT    [emphatic] [firm]        — the load-bearing claims ONLY. [emphatic]
            appears exactly 4 times; if a 5th gets added, none of them land.
  ACCENT    [curious] [slowly]
            [brighter] [calm] [warmly] — one-off turns.

DENSITY FOLLOWS THE RETENTION CHECKPOINTS. 23 tags, but they are not evenly
spread: 4 tags across the 4 hook lines, 8 across the glide-path reveal
(scenes 25-36), and long untagged runs through the setup and the checklist.
Bare lines are what make the tagged ones audible.

THE FOUR THAT MATTER MOST, and why:
  L02 [quietly] replaces [whispers]. A v3 whisper drops below the mix and Shorts
      play through phone speakers in public at low volume. If line 2 is inaudible
      the hook does not connect. Same intimacy, no dropout risk.
  L18 [slowly] on "Alcohol. Gambling. Tobacco. Weapons." — the most loaded line
      in the script had NO tag. One word per beat is the whole moment.
  L36 [brighter] replaces [calm] on "So. Two minutes. Three steps." [calm] after
      a heavy reveal reads as deflation. This is the gear change that converts
      dread into a save.
  L41 [slowly] on the sharia line — previously untagged, and it is the ONLY line
      in the video that pays the title's promise. Retention checkpoint 4 says it
      "must be understood, not just heard"; slowing it is the literal fix.

MECHANICS. One tag per line, never two. Tags at line start only. Ellipses still
carry the breath pauses — do not add [pause], v3 handles the ellipsis better.

FALLBACK. If v3 speaks a tag aloud instead of performing it, swap and re-render
that line only: [matter-of-fact]→[calm], [flat]→[serious], [quietly]→[softly],
[firm]→[serious], [brighter]→[warmly], [slowly]→ellipses between the words.
Test-render lines 1-4 and 29-36 before committing the full script.

PACE. 312 words (315 before the scene-04 copy fix). At the delivery this pack
was written for it lands near 165s (~113 wpm) — slow for the platform. The hook
is now 18 words; run it at ~135 wpm, roughly 8s for the four lines, not 14.
Target 130-140 wpm overall, land near 145s, and
spend the recovered time entirely on the glide-path reveal (scenes 25-36) rather
than spreading it evenly. Do not cut words to get there.

                  highlighting. No scaling or bouncing.
EMPHASIS        : italics only
TIMING          : swap on the speaker's natural clause break — the caption change
                  and the picture cut land together

NOTE ON CONTRAST: the black caption boxes are deliberately kept from the previous
spec. Against this warm cream palette they are the highest-contrast element on
screen, which is what keeps captions legible on a phone in daylight. Do not
restyle them to match the illustration — captions are UI, not artwork.

"Auto-dubbed" chip, and it is free additional reach.

EDIT & ASSEMBLY
================================================================================

1. Record the VO first. The cut follows speech cadence, so scene timings will
   shift to match the actual delivery. Treat the timecodes above as a guide.
2. Generate scene 02 as a style test and check it against Kareem's sheet before
   generating anything else. Then generate Adam's eight scenes as one block.
3. Attach the character sheet as an image reference on EVERY character scene.
4. HARD CUTS ONLY. No dissolves, no whip pans, no zoom transitions, no glitch.
5. Slow push-in or lateral drift on every scene. Nothing fully static.
6. NO film grain on this style. Grain was right for the 3D low-poly look and is

Your pension owns things you would never buy with your own money. Nobody asked
you — you were opted in, and that included what it buys.

Pension, 401(k), workplace plan: same machine. When you joined you chose a
number — how much goes in. You almost certainly never chose where it goes. That
is the default fund, and most people never move out of it.

What is in it? An index. A basket of the largest listed companies, bought
automatically by size, with no screening at all. That is not a flaw in an index
tracker — it is the definition of one. So the basket holds banks and insurers as
one of its biggest slices, alongside alcohol, gambling, tobacco and weapons.

The banks are the sharp part. A bank's product is not a service, it is interest.
Own the bank and you own the income. That is not a technicality, it is the whole
business.

And here is the part almost nobody knows. Your fund is built to change as you
age — "lifestyling", or a "glide path". It sounds like safety. What it actually
does is sell shares and buy bonds as you approach retirement. A bond is a loan.
You are lending money at interest. The machine moves you deeper into it the
older you get, automatically, as a feature you were never shown.

THE TWO-MINUTE CHECK:

1. Log in and find the name of your fund. If it says "default", "balanced", or a
   retirement year, that is the basket.
2. Search that fund's largest holdings — they are published. You will recognise
   the banks immediately.
3. Open the full fund list and look for a sharia or Islamic option. Most
   workplace schemes have one now, and switching is usually free.

You were opted in by default. Default is still a choice — somebody else just
