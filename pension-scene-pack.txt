  as working on this channel. Read 2026-08-08: JB6Q_7IE9Jo (Paper Gold, that
  format) ran ~13.9 v/day against its designed control 4gRoTTZNnFE
  (curiosity-gap) at ~6.2 — roughly 2.2x. Small, but it is the first readable
  packaging difference the channel has produced.

  entered — inflation, banks, insurance, mortgage, and now credit cards — has
  collapsed by entry 3 or 4. Credit cards took THREE entries in 42 hours on
  Aug 6-7 and produced 6, 37 and 19 views.

  top performer is NINE years old, and the two IFG entries are five. Every
  working viewer has one of these and most have never opened it.
- Length ~165s sits inside the 133-180s band that produced four of this
  channel's five best videos. DO NOT cut it to 60s. That rule was tested and
  found backwards — see next-slate.md rule 5.
- It is the first upload since Aug 7 18:00Z. Rule 0 — never let the Shorts gap
  exceed ~1 day — is the only production rule on this channel with real evidence
  behind it, and it is the one currently at risk.

================================================================================
BRAND-SAFETY NOTE — read before generating
================================================================================

No prompt in this pack names or depicts a real pension provider, fund manager,
index, bank, or any real wordmark. Every statement, dashboard and fund list on
screen is GENERIC and unbranded. The video names no product and recommends none.

This is deliberate and it is the channel's own editorial rule, set in the
credit-card Short: "I'm deliberately not naming a product. Terms change,
availability changes, and a video that names one is out of date the month it
does. The test doesn't change." Same here. The video teaches the CHECK, not a
fund. That is also what makes it a template you can re-run.

A generated lookalike logo is both a trademark problem and an AI-artefact
magnet. Keep every screen generic.

Pension, 401(k), workplace plan: same machine. When you joined you chose a
number — how much goes in. You almost certainly never chose where it goes. That
is the default fund, and most people never move out of it.

What is in it? An index. A basket of the largest listed companies, bought
automatically by size, with no screening at all. That is not a flaw in an index
tracker — it is the definition of one. So the basket holds banks and insurers as
one of its biggest slices, alongside alcohol, gambling, tobacco and weapons.

The banks are the sharp part. A bank's product is not a service, it is interest.
Own the bank and you own the income. That is not a technicality, it is the whole
business.

And here is the part almost nobody knows. Your fund is built to change as you
age — "lifestyling", or a "glide path". It sounds like safety. What it actually
does is sell shares and buy bonds as you approach retirement. A bond is a loan.
You are lending money at interest. The machine moves you deeper into it the
older you get, automatically, as a feature you were never shown.
