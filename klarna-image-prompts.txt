WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY

1. TOKEN COST. The Track A/B prompts repeat the same 60 words of style and
   costume in every single prompt. Here that is hoisted into two blocks you
   paste once. Each scene prompt is then ~45 words instead of ~115 — a little
   over half the tokens per generation, and the look holds better because the
   style string is byte-identical every time instead of paraphrased.

2. FULL SCRIPT COVERAGE. Five scenes (02, 05, 06, 07, 11) run 5.8–6.5s and
   carry TWO ideas each. One still could not cover both, so the late fee and
   "that's the actual product" had no frame at all — they were captions over an
   unrelated picture. Those five scenes now get two stills each, cut on the
   scene's own CAP 2 in-point. 11 scenes, 16 frames, every VO line pictured.

3. NO FRAME HOLDS PAST ~3.3s. A 6.53s static frame in a Short is a scroll.
   Longest hold here is 4.73s (the hook, which has to breathe) and 4.37s (the
   rule, deliberately locked). Everything else sits between 2.9s and 3.5s.

HARD CONSTRAINT — THE EDIT MUST FINISH UNDER 60 SECONDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured on this channel's own catalogue (inventory run 30883075618, 2026-08-04),
every public Short past its 72-hour test, normalised to views/day:

  31–60s   n=1    median 89.8 v/day
  61–90s   n=3    median  6.9 v/day   <-- no survivors
  >90s     n=11   median 42.4 v/day

61–90s is the only bucket on the channel with nothing in it that worked. The cut
lands at 55.37s, which clears it by 4.63 seconds and no more. So: no end card, no
extended hook, no breathing room at the tail. If the edit grows, it must be
trimmed back under 60 — not allowed to drift to 62.

The prompt pack cites "slate rule: 30–60s" as the reason for this length. That
rule was retired on 2026-08-03 as unevidenced (see next-slate.md rule 5). The
length is right; the stated reason is stale. Do not re-derive anything from it.

The scene boundaries are untouched — beats.ts, the VO and the caption table all
still line up. The five new cuts happen inside scenes, so nothing upstream moves.

--- F01 · 0:00.0 · THE HOOK ---------------------------------------------------
85mm macro, f/2.8. A phone lies face up on a dark brushed-charcoal counter, its
screen showing a blank checkout panel — pale grey card, four small empty rounded
boxes in a row, nothing written anywhere. Two simplified geometric hands in
terracotta #E85F42 cuffs rest at the frame edge, one fingertip touching the
counter. Low raking key, one long defined shadow thrown right, fine dust and
micro-scratches on the counter, warm amber highlight along the phone's edge.
Phone composed low, the top two-fifths of the frame empty.

--- F02A · 0:04.7 · FOUR PAYMENTS ---------------------------------------------
50mm, f/4, shot perpendicular to the surface. Overhead flat-lay of four
identical blank cream cards laid in a precise even row on dark matte charcoal,
generous equal spacing, each casting an identical hard shadow. Near-
photorealistic paper grain and deckled edges. Nothing else in frame. Square-on,
generous margin all round.

--- F02B · 0:07.9 · SO WHO PAYS THE FIFTH -------------------------------------
50mm, f/4, overhead flat-lay pulled slightly wider. The same four blank cream
cards sit to the left. To the right of the fourth, one empty card-sized gap
holding only a clean rectangular impression in the dust where a fifth card
should be — the surface bare, the outline unmistakable. Single hard overhead key
slightly off-axis so all four shadows fall the same way. The empty gap sits dead
centre of frame.
  [ The video track revealed a fifth shadow with no card above it. A still can't
    stage that — a shadow without an object reads as a render fault. The dust
    impression says the same thing and is physically photographable. ]

--- F03 · 0:11.1 · THE FEE   ** GENERATE FIRST — shopkeeper reference ** -------
35mm, f/2. Environmental portrait from the customer's side of a shop counter.
The SHOPKEEPER stands behind it, both forearms flat on the counter, weight
settled, completely still. A short stack of banknotes sits on the counter in
front of him, the top note slightly offset from the rest. Near-photorealistic
shop behind, shelving falling into soft focus. Warm practical overhead light plus
a cooler window fill from the left. Counter edge sharp, background soft.

--- F04 · 0:15.2 · WHERE IT GOES ----------------------------------------------
100mm macro, f/2.8. A blank cream cardboard price tag hangs by string from the
cuff of a folded dark wool jacket. The tag is completely blank — no printing.
Heavy card stock, visible fibre at the punched hole, one soft crease. Hard low
raking key throws a long angular shadow of the tag across the wool behind it,
warm amber rim tracing its top edge. Extremely shallow focus: the tag face sharp,
the wool dissolving within centimetres. Tag low-centre, empty dark space filling
the upper half.

--- F05A · 0:18.7 · THE PRICE WENT UP FOR EVERYONE ----------------------------
28mm, f/4, camera at chest height dead centre. Deep one-point-perspective shot
down a clothing shop aisle, rails of muted garments receding on both sides so
they converge symmetrically, dozens of small blank cream tags hanging along them
at irregular heights, all unprinted. Cool overhead fluorescent wash with a single
warm amber pool at the far end drawing the eye through. Nothing human in frame.
Vanishing point set low so the upper third stays open and dark.

--- F05B · 0:21.6 · INCLUDING THE PERSON PAYING CASH --------------------------
35mm, f/2.8, same aisle from further back. At the far end, alone at the till,
the CASH BUYER stands small in frame with folded banknotes held in one hand and
no phone anywhere near them. The rest of the shop is empty. Foreground rails
soft and dark, the warm amber pool falling on the figure so it is the only lit
thing in the frame. Figure low and slightly off centre, deep dark above.

--- F06A · 0:24.5 · THEY NEVER USED IT ----------------------------------------
85mm, f/2. Close three-quarter shot of the CASH BUYER's hands at the shop
counter, cropped at the chest with no head in frame, holding three worn banknotes
mid-count, the top note lifted slightly clear of the others. Counter surface
scuffed and real, warm side light from camera-left, deep shadow filling the right
of the frame. Near-photorealistic paper texture, soft folds and worn corners on
the notes. Notes sharp, coat and background soft. Quiet, slightly melancholy.

--- F06B · 0:27.5 · THEN THE LATE FEE -----------------------------------------
85mm macro, f/2.8. A phone lies face up on the same scuffed counter, screen
almost black and glass-clean except for one blank pale notification panel
floating near the top — an empty rounded card, no text, no icons, no symbols.
The cold screen light spills a small hard pool onto the counter and everything
beyond it falls to near-black. Phone low-centre, dark space above.
  [ The one deliberately cold frame in the film — no amber accent. It is the
    sting, and the temperature break is what makes it land. Keep the rest warm. ]

--- F07A · 0:30.4 · THE BIGGER BASKET -----------------------------------------
50mm, f/4, slightly high angle. A wire shopping basket on a dark matte floor,
overfull — folded clothing, a plain shoe box, a small carton stacked past the rim
with one item balanced precariously on the edge, about to fall but not falling.
Near-photorealistic cardboard and fabric texture, one hard key from upper left
throwing a defined basket-weave shadow across the floor. Muted cream and sand
palette. Basket low-centre with a clear diagonal of empty floor leading out of
frame.

--- F07B · 0:33.6 · THAT'S THE ACTUAL PRODUCT ---------------------------------
50mm, f/5.6, square-on. The same overfull wire basket, restaged as a product
hero: centred on a low dark plinth, lit like a museum object in a single tight
pool of light, everything beyond the plinth black. Deliberate, reverent,
absolutely nothing else in frame. Basket centred low, wide dark margin above.

--- F08 · 0:36.9 · THE RULE ---------------------------------------------------
60mm, f/5.6, locked-off tabletop still life on a dark walnut surface with visible
grain. A single blank cream card lies flat and perfectly square to frame in the
centre. A heavy brass stamp rests beside it, face down, weight evident. Nothing
else in frame. Near-photorealistic brass with real specular hits and fine patina,
one long unbroken shadow to the right. Absolute symmetry and stillness — the
composition should feel settled and final. Objects centred low, wide dark margin
above.

--- F09 · 0:41.3 · MURABAHA ---------------------------------------------------
50mm, f/5.6, shot square-on. Museum-style still life on a dark matte plinth: a
folded wool jacket sits on the right, a single heavy brass bar resting beside it,
the two objects clearly separated, with empty plinth to the left where the jacket
used to be. Near-photorealistic brass and wool texture, real contact shadows
anchoring each object, warm amber rim on the brass. Deliberate, weighted, almost
reverent staging. Objects in the lower two-thirds.

--- F10 · 0:46.1 · SAME SHAPE, BUILT WRONG ------------------------------------
100mm extreme macro, f/4. A single cream payment card stands upright on a dark
matte surface, identical in form and colour to the stamped card in F08. A fine
fracture runs up its face from the bottom edge and stops halfway, the card
otherwise intact and standing. Visible paper fibre and lifted burr along the
break. Hard raking light strikes across the fracture so the split throws its own
micro-shadow. Card low-centre, dark above.

--- F11A · 0:49.2 · THE CLOSE   ** GENERATE EARLY — AMIR reference ** ----------
35mm, f/2.8, low three-quarter. AMIR stands just outside a shop doorway in warm
low evening sun, one small paper bag held at his side, mid-stride but weight
settled. Near-photorealistic street behind him, quiet and empty, long golden-hour
shadows stretching toward camera. Warm amber backlight rimming his shoulders,
cool ambient fill on the front. Street soft behind. Calm, resolved, unhurried.
Figure in the lower two-thirds, generous open sky above.

--- F11B · 0:52.3 · FOLLOW ----------------------------------------------------
35mm, f/2.8, low and wide, from behind. AMIR walks away down the same quiet
street, small in frame, the paper bag at his side, his long shadow trailing back
toward camera. Golden hour, the shop front soft behind him, the street empty.
Figure small and low-centre, the upper half of the frame open sky held clear for
the closing captions.
