==============================================================================
FULL VOICEOVER  (continuous read)
==============================================================================

   Wells Fargo took your biggest payment first. On purpose.

   Say you have a hundred dollars and four transactions - three small ones,
   and your rent.

   In the order they actually happened, you overdraft once.

   Wells Fargo reordered them highest-first, so rent drained the account and
   all three small purchases bounced behind it.

   One overdraft became four. Thirty-five dollars each.

   A judge found an internal memo predicting the change would earn them forty
   million dollars a year.

   The court ordered two hundred and three million returned.

>> They called it high-to-low reordering.

   It wasn't a glitch. It was the product.

==============================================================================
VERIFIED
==============================================================================

  Gutierrez v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. - Judge William Alsup, N.D. Cal., August 2010.
  Wells Fargo posted debit transactions highest-first rather than chronologically,
  which drew balances down faster and multiplied overdraft occurrences.
  The opinion cites an internal memo predicting roughly +$40M a year in extra fees.
  $203M ordered returned in restitution. Judgment final after appeals, 4 April 2016.
