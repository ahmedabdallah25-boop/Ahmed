WHY THIS ONE, AND WHY NOW
================================================================================

- THE SOURCE IS A PROVEN SHAPE, NOT A GUESS. KykVGTsqeEI: 18,154 views and 1,010
  likes = a 5.6% like rate on a 27-minute video about Greek grammar. That is an
  extraordinary ratio for the length. People do not sit through 27 minutes of
  word study unless the emotional frame is carrying them, and the frame is what
  is being borrowed here.

- THE ISLAMIC TWIN IS UNUSUALLY EXACT. Most reverse-engineering forces the
  analogy. This one does not have to:

    Romans 8:28                        →  Ash-Sharḥ 94:5-6
    the most-quoted, most-misused verse →  the most-quoted, most-misused āyah
    Greek `synergeo`                    →  Arabic `maʿa` — WITH, not after
    "all things" ≠ each thing is good   →  definite al-ʿusr / indefinite yusran
    v29 defines "good", one verse on    →  94:7-8 define the ease, one āyah on
    Paul wrote it from prison           →  revealed in the fatrah, the worst season
    the Roman arch                      →  the pointed arch of a masjid arcade
    Joseph in the pit                   →  Yūsuf ؑ in the well, Mūsā's mother

  Every one of those is a structural match, not a decorative one. The source's
  spine survives the transplant intact, which is the whole reason this subject
  was chosen over any other āyah.

- THE DEMAND IS THE SAME DEMAND. "Indeed with hardship comes ease" is the single
  most-forwarded line in any Muslim group chat after a death. Everyone receiving
  this video has already been handed the āyah at a bad moment. The video's job is
  to give them back the half they were not sent.

- THE SUPPLY IS THE WRONG SHAPE — the same gap the finance channel exploits.
  The existing content on this āyah is almost entirely short reminders and
  recitation-over-nasheed edits. Almost none of it does the ARABIC. That is the
  gap, and it is exactly the gap "Deep Made Simple" found in English Bible study.

STYLE LOCK — the grammar every prompt below is written in
================================================================================

MEDIUM. Hand-painted watercolour with fine ink linework, on cold-press paper with
visible tooth and grain. Soft wet-edge blooms where colours meet. Pigment pools
slightly at the edges of washes. The ink line is loose and slightly broken — it
does not trace every shape, and it never has an even machine weight. Areas of
bare paper are left unpainted and are part of the composition.

LIGHT. Soft, diffused, directionless daylight. Low contrast throughout. No harsh
shadow, no dramatic rim light, no glow. Shadows are a cool grey-violet wash, not
black.

DEPTH. Shallow. Foreground subject rendered with more line detail; backgrounds
dissolve into loose wash with almost no line. Nothing is sharp edge-to-edge.

FRAME. 16:9 landscape, 1920x1080. Compose with generous negative space in the
UPPER THIRD or one side — that space is where the typeset Arabic and translation
land in the edit. Never fill the frame.

PALETTE — six colours, nothing else.

  | Role                       | Hex       |
  |----------------------------|-----------|
  | Lapis blue (primary)       | #1F3A93   |
  | Burnt sienna (earth, skin) | #C1663F   |
  | Saffron ochre (light, gold)| #C68A1E   |
  | Verdigris sage (life)      | #6E8B74   |
  | Limestone cream (paper/bg) | #EFE4CE   |
  | Slate ink (line, shadow)   | #3A3F4B   |

  Lapis carries grief, night, water and the unknown. Saffron ochre carries
  revelation, lamp-light and Fajr. Those two are the argument in colour: the
  video ends by putting ochre INSIDE a lapis frame rather than after it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TWO HARD RULES. Both are non-negotiable and both differ from the source video.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RULE 1 — NO FACE IS EVER RESOLVED. NO PROPHET IS EVER DEPICTED.

  The source video shows a recurring woman's face, and shows Paul, Joseph and a
  crucifix. None of that transfers.

  - No prophet — not Muḥammad ﷺ, not Yūsuf, not Mūsā — is depicted in any form,
    including from behind, in silhouette, or as a shape. Their scenes are carried
    by THE PLACE and THE OBJECT: the well without anyone in it, the empty
    riverbank, the basket, the reed. This is why chapters 3 and 11 are shot the
    way they are below.
  - The recurring modern character is always: from behind, over the shoulder,
    cropped above the mouth, turned away, or reflected out of focus. Never a
    resolved face.

  Read this as a design decision, not a restriction. A face you never see is why
  the viewer keeps occupying the frame themselves — which is the whole point of
  a second-person script. The source had to work to get that effect. This gets it
  for free.

RULE 2 — NO IMAGE MODEL EVER RENDERS ARABIC. NOT ONCE.

  Every image generator produces broken, meaningless Arabic — wrong letterforms,
  unjoined glyphs, invented characters. On a video whose entire argument is that
  the exact Arabic wording matters, one malformed āyah on screen destroys the
  channel's credibility permanently, and the comments will be about nothing else.

  So: every prompt below that needs Arabic asks the model for an EMPTY cartouche,
  banner, panel or plaque — a blank illuminated shape. The Arabic is typeset in
  the editor, on top, in a real font.

    Arabic       : KFGQPC Uthmanic Script HAFS, or Amiri Quran
    Translation  : EB Garamond, or Cormorant Garamond
    Transliteration: EB Garamond Italic

  This applies to English lettering too. Nothing in this pack is rendered as text
  by the model. Everything legible is typeset.

PLATE DENSITY — the one place this pack is measurably thinner than the source
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured, not asserted — because an earlier draft of this file claimed ~112
on-screen changes and that was simply wrong:

    THIS PACK    76 plates over 1,479s  =  19.5s average hold
    THE SOURCE  ~115 images over 1,618s =  14.1s average hold

  To match the source's cutting rhythm you would need ~105 plates, not 76.
  26 of the 76 plates here hold longer than 20s.

  That is a real deviation, and you should decide about it now rather than
  discover it in the edit. Two defensible positions:

  KEEP 76. The frames here are calmer and the VO is denser than the source's.
  The long holds on scenes 19, 43, 63 and 65 are doing deliberate work, and
  cutting them would hurt. 19.5s is slow, but not sleepy when the Ken Burns move
  is live underneath it.

  GO TO ~105. If test viewers report drift, split the longest holds. In priority
  order: scenes 30 (36s), 37 (29s), 29 (28s), 19 (27s), 36 (26s), 74 (25s),
  16 (24s), 18 (24s), 55 (24s), 23 (22s). Each splits naturally at a VO sentence
  break into two plates of the SAME subject at different scale — a wide and a
  detail. That is exactly how the source reaches its density without inventing
  new subjects, and it costs you no new ideas.

  DO NOT split scenes 19, 43, 63 or 65 whatever else you do. Those four holds
  are the video.

THE RECURRING CAST — three devices, no faces
================================================================================

THE ONE GRIEVING — the viewer's stand-in, in every chapter
  A woman in her thirties. Soft grey-blue hijab, loosely wrapped, a little
  undone. Long charcoal wool coat, sleeves slightly too long. Always shot from
  BEHIND, over the shoulder, from the side cropped above the mouth, or as an
  out-of-focus reflection. Her hands are the expressive part — generate them
  carefully, they carry every emotional beat her face cannot.
  She is the direct equivalent of the source's recurring modern woman, and she
  also closes the gap house-style.md flags: "No female characters in the cast."

THE LAMP — the recurring object
  A small clay oil lamp with a single flame, sometimes in a wall niche. It
  appears in eleven plates. It is never explained on screen. It starts in
  darkness (ch.1), is nearly out (ch.3), and is still burning at Fajr (ch.13).
  Do not draw attention to it; let it accumulate.

THE ARCH — the structural motif
  A pointed masonry arch. Appears as a fragment early (plates 12, 34), unbuilt
  in ch.10, and complete at the close. This is the source's Roman arch, rebuilt.

RECURRING SETTING VOCABULARY
  Limestone walls, date palms, still water, reed matting, folded cloth, a plain
  prayer mat, sand, empty doorways, the horizon.

ACCURACY NOTES — every claim, and how hard it holds
================================================================================
The full table is in ash-sharh-script.md. The four that decide whether this video
survives contact with a knowledgeable audience:

1. "ONE HARDSHIP WILL NEVER OVERCOME TWO EASES" IS NOT PRESENTED AS A ḤADĪTH.
   It is reported from Ibn ʿAbbās, and from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī as a marfūʿ report
   with a broken chain. The script says exactly that, on camera, and scene 37
   exists to make the caveat un-cuttable. This is the single highest-risk claim
   in the video and it is the one most often overstated by other content on this
   āyah. Do not "tighten" it.

2. THE DEFINITE/INDEFINITE READING IS GIVEN AS THE MAJORITY TAFSĪR READING, NOT
   THE ONLY ONE. Chapter 6 names the competing reading (repetition as pure
   taʾkīd) before using the grammarians' one. Keep that.

3. MAʿA IS NOT CLAIMED AS STRICT SIMULTANEITY. Chapter 5 concedes openly that
   many mufassirūn read it as nearness rather than same-instant, then shows that
   even the conservative reading rules out the queue. The concession is what makes
   the argument credible rather than clever. Keep that too.

4. NO PROPHET IS DEPICTED, AND ﷺ FOLLOWS EVERY MENTION. Both in the script and in
   all 76 plates. Check the final render frame by frame for this before publish;
   an image model asked for "a well" will sometimes add a figure unprompted.

DELIBERATELY NOT SAID:
  · That suffering is sent in order to teach you. Chapter 7 explicitly refuses it.
  · That grief should end by any particular time.
  · Any timeline promise of any kind. "It gets better" appears nowhere.

DESCRIPTION
  Everyone sends you this āyah. Almost nobody sends you the two lines that say
  what it means.

  "With hardship comes ease" is one of the most quoted lines in the Qurʾān, and
  one of the most misread. This study goes word by word through Sūrah ash-Sharḥ —
  what maʿa actually means, why it is not baʿda, why the sentence is said twice,
  and why the definition of the "ease" is sitting one āyah past where most people
  stop reading.

  The sūrah was revealed to a man in the worst season of his life, during a
  silence he was being taunted for. He was not spared afterwards. If the āyah
  promised comfortable circumstances, the first man to receive it would disprove
  it. What it actually promises is deeper, and it is the reason it survives a
  graveside.
