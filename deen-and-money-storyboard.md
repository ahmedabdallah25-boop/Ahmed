## 1. Directorial decisions (read this first)

**Format: Shorts-first, 75–90 seconds.** The sister channel's five 900+ view winners were all Shorts.
The one long-form upload sits at 2 views. At 0 subs there is no Browse signal to feed long-form — the
Shorts feed is the only cold-start surface that exists. Build the audience on Shorts; the long-form
expansions are noted per episode for when the channel clears ~1k subs.

**Differentiation from Finance % Decoded.** Two channels in one niche will cannibalise each other unless
they own different jobs. Split them like this:

| | Finance % Decoded | **Deen & Money** |
|---|---|---|
| Job | *How the machine works* | *What it costs you, to the dollar* |
| Device | Mechanism diagrams | **Live arithmetic — numbers count up on screen** |
| Proof | Explanation | A running total the viewer can check |
| Signature | "No jargon, just mechanisms." | **"Run the numbers. Then run them halal."** |

Every Deen & Money video is built around **one number that grows on screen** while the character
watches it. That is the channel's visual signature and the reason to subscribe.

**Script law — the Breakthrough Spine.** Reverse-engineered from the five winners (Parts 8, 3, 9, 10, 4)
and from why Parts 11–12 stalled. Every script below obeys it:

| Beat | Time | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Hook** | 0:00–0:04 | Second person + an impossible-sounding claim about *the viewer's own money*. No metaphor, no series bookkeeping, no Arabic term. The sting lands in line one — not line four. |
| 2. **Stakes** | 0:04–0:15 | Name the everyday thing this is about. Still 100% general — a non-Muslim viewer must feel targeted. |
| 3. **The math** | 0:15–0:45 | The number appears and grows. This is the retention engine. |
| 4. **The turn** | 0:45–0:55 | "This is only true because of *how* it's structured. Structure it differently and the math inverts." The pivot sentence — never announce religion here, announce *design*. |
| 5. **The dive** | 0:55–1:15 | Now the Islamic finance mechanism, named, with the same number re-run. Arabic term used once, in plain English. |
| 6. **Close** | 1:15–1:25 | Forward tease (next episode's number) + signature line. |

**Title formula:** `How to [benefit] Without [pain]` or `Your [thing] [does harm]`. Arabic term goes in a
`(X Explained)` suffix only — branding for warm viewers, never the hook for cold ones.

**Publish window:** 3:00–4:30 AM PT — the proven window on the sister channel. Daily for the first 10
uploads, then settle to the weekly cadence the About page promises.

**Title:** Two People Saved the Same $200. One Retired With 4x More.
**Runtime:** 84s · **Number:** $200/month · **Mechanism:** Shariah-screened equity, profit-sharing (mudarabah)

### Script

> **[HOOK — 0:00]**
> Two people saved the exact same two hundred dollars a month. For thirty years. Same job, same

> one took a bigger risk.

> looking.
>
> **[THE MATH — 0:20]**
> Person A put it in a savings account. Two hundred a month, thirty years, four percent. That's
> seventy-two thousand of their own money in — and about eighty-six thousand out. Thirty years of
> saving bought them fourteen thousand dollars.

> deposits. Same thirty years. Around three hundred and forty thousand.

> **[THE TURN — 0:50]**

>

> This is the exact split Islamic finance is built on. You're not allowed to rent out money —

> mudarabah: your capital, their work, split the profit — and split the loss too. That's not a
> loophole. That's the whole reason the returns are different. You stopped being a creditor and
> started being an owner.

# EPISODE 3 — "Your Credit Card Is Designed to Take 22 Years"

**Title:** Your Credit Card Is Designed to Take 22 Years (Minimum Payments Explained)
**Runtime:** 86s · **Number:** $5,000 → $12,700 · **Mechanism:** Qard Hasan

### Script

> **[HOOK — 0:00]**
> If you owe five thousand dollars and you pay the minimum every single month — on time, never
> late, never missing once — you will be paying that card in twenty-two years. That's not a
> punishment for being irresponsible. That's what happens when you follow the instructions.
>
> **[STAKES — 0:14]**
> The minimum payment feels like the safe option. It's printed on the statement like advice. It
> isn't advice. It's the product.
>
> **[THE MATH — 0:24]**
> Five thousand dollars at twenty-two percent. Minimum payment: two percent of the balance, about a
> hundred dollars.
> Of that hundred, ninety-one is interest. Nine dollars touches the debt.
> Next month the balance is barely lower, so the minimum drops too — and the whole thing stretches.
> Total paid over twenty-two years: about twelve thousand seven hundred dollars. On five thousand
> borrowed. You will hand over seven thousand seven hundred dollars for the privilege of waiting.
>
> **[THE TURN — 0:52]**
> Look at what the lender is actually selling. Not the money — you already had access to the money.
> They're selling *delay*, and they priced it so the delay never ends. A contract where the safest
> behaviour produces the worst outcome isn't a contract. It's a trap with a payment schedule.
>
> **[THE DIVE — 1:04]**
> Islamic finance doesn't reform this — it deletes the category. Lending money at a price is riba,
> full stop, and the reason is precisely this: the lender takes no risk, does no work, owns nothing,
> and still earns more the longer you struggle. The only permitted loan is qard hasan — a good loan.
> You lend a thousand, you get back a thousand. Not a cent more, ever, no matter how long it takes.
> The upside for the lender is entirely non-financial. Which means the moment you're in trouble, the
> lender has nothing to gain from you staying there.
> And practically: pay the highest rate first, kill the balance, never let the minimum decide.
>
> **[CLOSE — 1:22]**
> Next: the mortgage that costs you two houses.

# EPISODE 4 — "A 30-Year Mortgage Costs You Two Houses"

**Title:** How to Buy a House Without Paying for Two (Diminishing Musharakah)
**Runtime:** 88s · **Number:** $400,000 → $859,000 · **Mechanism:** Diminishing Musharakah

### Script

> **[HOOK — 0:00]**
> You will buy two houses in your lifetime. You will live in one. You will never see the other one,
> never own it, and never be able to sell it — but you'll pay full price.
>
> **[STAKES — 0:10]**
> Four hundred thousand dollar house. Twenty percent down. Thirty-year mortgage at six and a half
> percent. This is the most normal transaction in the developed world.
>
> **[THE MATH — 0:20]**
> You borrow three hundred and twenty thousand. Your payment is two thousand and twenty-three
> dollars a month. Thirty years is three hundred and sixty payments — seven hundred and
> twenty-eight thousand dollars.
> Add your eighty thousand down. Total out of your pocket: eight hundred and eight thousand for a
> four hundred thousand dollar house.
> And here's the part that stings. In year one, of that two-thousand-dollar payment, about
> seventeen hundred and thirty is interest. Two hundred and ninety-three dollars buys house.
> You have to make payments for nearly nineteen years before the majority of a single payment goes
> toward the thing you're actually buying.
>
> **[THE TURN — 0:58]**
> Ask the obvious question: what did the bank contribute? Not the house — the seller had that. Not
> the risk — if you default, they take the house and you eat the loss. They contributed the money,
> and they charged you for the calendar again, four hundred thousand dollars' worth.
>
> **[THE DIVE — 1:10]**
> So change what the bank is. Not a lender — a *partner*. You put in twenty percent, they put in
> eighty, and you both own the house in those proportions, on paper, from day one. You live in it,
> so you pay rent — but only on the share you don't own yet. And every month you also buy another
> slice of their share. Their ownership shrinks, yours grows, and the rent falls with it. That's
> diminishing musharakah.
> Two consequences that matter: nothing compounds, and if the house burns down, the partner loses
> their share too — because they actually own it. Risk sits with ownership. That's the entire
> principle.
>
> **[CLOSE — 1:30]**
> Next: why your five percent raise made you poorer.
> Run the numbers. Then run them halal.

# EPISODE 5 — "Your 5% Raise Made You Poorer"

**Title:** Your 5% Raise Made You Poorer. Here's the Arithmetic.
**Runtime:** 80s · **Number:** $60,000 → $57,900 real · **Mechanism:** Sound money / real assets

### Script

> **[HOOK — 0:00]**
> You got a five percent raise this year. You are poorer than you were last year. Both of those
> are true at the same time, and the second one is the one that shows up in your groceries.
>
> **[STAKES — 0:11]**
> Sixty thousand a year. Five percent raise takes you to sixty-three. It felt like a win. You
> probably told someone.
>
> **[THE MATH — 0:20]**
> Inflation that year: eight and a half percent. Not the number in the headline — the number on the
> things you actually buy: rent, food, fuel, insurance.
> Sixty-three thousand dollars, buying at last year's prices, is worth about fifty-eight thousand.
> You didn't get a three-thousand-dollar raise. You took a two-thousand-dollar pay cut and got a
> thank-you note.
> And it happens every year, quietly, whether or not anyone gives you a raise.
>
> **[THE TURN — 0:44]**
> Here's the part that changes how you see it. Inflation isn't weather. Nobody voted for it. It's
> what happens when the quantity of money grows faster than the quantity of things — and the
> people who create the money spend it first, at yesterday's prices, before it reaches you. By the
> time it gets to your paycheck, the prices have already moved. You're always last in that queue.
>
> **[THE DIVE — 0:58]**
> This is why Islamic finance has never treated money as a product. Money is a *measure* — you
> don't sell the ruler. Classical Islamic money was a weight of a real thing, the gold dinar and
> the silver dirham. A weight can't be printed. That's the whole point.
> You can't reissue the currency by yourself. What you can do is stop holding your life savings in
> the unit that's being diluted: own things that reprice — real assets, ownership stakes,
> Shariah-screened equity, metals. Zakat pushes the same direction, by design — two and a half
> percent a year on idle hoarded wealth, zero on productive assets. The system taxes stagnation
> and rewards deployment.
> Sitting in cash isn't safe. It's just slow.
>
> **[CLOSE — 1:16]**
> Next season, we run these numbers on your salary, month by month.
> Run the numbers. Then run them halal.

## 4. Production notes

**Consistency across frames.** Generate Kareem's reference sheet first (prompt in §2), then pass it as an
image reference on every shot alongside the written CHARACTER BLOCK. Text-only prompts will drift on the
beard and the henley collar within about six frames.

**On-screen text is the retention engine.** Every number spoken must appear as text within 0.3s of being
said. Big, top third, high contrast — most Shorts viewers are on mute for the first two seconds.

**The hook frame is the thumbnail.** Shot 1 of each episode must read at 168px with no text at all: two
cars, two houses, two identical men. Silhouette-level clarity.

**Do not open with series bookkeeping.** No "in part one we covered…" — that beat is what halved the
sister channel's velocity on Parts 11–12. At 0 subscribers, ~100% of impressions are cold viewers.

**Arabic terms:** exactly once per script, after the 55-second mark, always immediately followed by the
plain-English definition. Never in the hook, never in the first line of the title.

**Numbers:** all figures above are illustrative and rounded — re-verify against a current amortisation
calculator before recording, and keep the "financial education, not financial advice" line in every
description.

**Ship order:** 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5, daily at 3:00–4:30 AM PT. Apply the sister channel's 72-hour rule: if
two consecutive episodes underperform the first, the problem is the hook's first four words, not the topic.
